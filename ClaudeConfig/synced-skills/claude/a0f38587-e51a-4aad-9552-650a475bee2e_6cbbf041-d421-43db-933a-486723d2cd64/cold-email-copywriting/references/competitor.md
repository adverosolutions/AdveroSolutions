# Cold Email Framework — Angle 4: Competitor

**Why it works:** Social proof + FOMO. If a direct competitor is already winning with something, that's one of the strongest believability levers Hormozi describes — proof from a peer beats a claim from a stranger every time.

---

## Variables

**Prospect**
- `{{firstName}}`, `{{companyName}}`, `{{industry}}`

**Research**
- `{{competitorName}}` — the competitor you're referencing
- `{{competitorResult}}` — the specific result/change tied to that competitor
- `{{painPoint}}` — the problem the competitor's result relates to

**Offer (fill in once your real business is locked in)**
- `{{leadMagnet}}` — the free breakdown/resource you're offering
- `{{mechanism}}` — optional, the "how" behind the competitor's result

**CTA**
- `{{cta}}` — the low-friction ask

---

## The 4-Part Skeleton

```
1. SUBJECT LINE — curiosity/relevance, 3–6 words, never salesy
2. THE OPENING (Hook) — one sentence proving specific homework, zero pitch
3. THE VALUE (Give) — free resource offered BEFORE any ask
4. LOW-FRICTION CTA — a yes/no question, not a calendar link
```

---

## Subject Line Formulas

- `how {{competitorName}} is handling {{painPoint}}`
- `{{competitorName}} vs {{companyName}} on {{painPoint}}`
- `quick competitive note`

---

## Framework Template

```
SUBJECT: how {{competitorName}} is handling {{painPoint}}

Hi {{firstName}},

Noticed {{competitorName}} recently {{competitorResult}} — and
it's largely because of how they're handling {{painPoint}}.

Put together a quick {{leadMagnet}} breaking down the approach
in case it's useful for {{companyName}} too, no strings attached.

Want me to send it over?

{{senderName}}
```

---

## Hormozi Principles Applied

| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Perceived Likelihood** (Value Equation) | Proof that this already worked for a peer makes the outcome far more believable than a claim about your own product. |
| **Give before you ask** ($100M Leads) | The breakdown is offered before any request for time. |
| **Specificity = believability** | `{{competitorResult}}` names an actual, verifiable change — not "they're doing great." |
| **Low-friction CTA** | "Want me to send it over?" not a call request. |

---

## A Note on Tact

Frame this as *useful intel*, not as trash-talking the competitor or implying the prospect is "losing." The tone should read as "thought this might be useful," never "you're behind."

---

## Filled Example

```
SUBJECT: how RedHawk Roofing is handling ad spend

Hi Mike,

Noticed RedHawk Roofing recently doubled down on Google Local
Service Ads — and it looks like it's paying off based on their
review volume picking up the last couple months.

Put together a quick breakdown of what that setup usually looks
like in case it's useful for Apex too, no strings attached.

Want me to send it over?

Jordan
```

---

## Tone Checklist

- [ ] Would this sound like a peer sharing intel, not a sales rep creating fear?
- [ ] No "I hope this email finds you well," no "I wanted to reach out."
- [ ] Contractions used naturally.
- [ ] One idea per sentence.
- [ ] Value is *offered*, not attached — ask "want me to send it?"
- [ ] Sign-off is first name only.
- [ ] Under 120 words total.

**Where to find competitor intel:** Google/Meta ad libraries, LinkedIn company updates, review site activity spikes, job postings mentioning specific tools, press releases.
