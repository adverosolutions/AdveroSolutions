# Cold Email Framework — Angle 1: Student / School Project

**Why it works:** Nobody has a defense mechanism against a student. It disarms the usual "what are you selling me" filter and gets genuinely high reply rates — but it only works if it's *true and specific*, not a gimmick.

---

## Variables

**Prospect**
- `{{firstName}}`, `{{companyName}}`, `{{website}}`, `{{industry}}`

**Research**
- `{{icebreaker}}` — specific thing you noticed about them (site, ad, product, post)
- `{{painPoint}}` — the specific problem your project/resource touches on

**Sender**
- `{{senderName}}`
- `{{senderSchool}}`
- `{{senderProgram}}` — e.g., "Marketing 401" or "Senior Capstone"
- `{{senderProject}}` — one-line description of the project

**Offer (fill in once your real business is locked in)**
- `{{leadMagnet}}` — the free resource/insight you're giving away
- `{{proofPoint}}` — optional, a stat or example backing the resource

**CTA**
- `{{cta}}` — the low-friction ask

---

## The 4-Part Skeleton

```
1. SUBJECT LINE — curiosity/relevance, 3–6 words, never salesy
2. THE OPENING (Hook) — one sentence proving specific homework, zero pitch
3. THE VALUE (Give) — free resource offered BEFORE any ask
4. LOW-FRICTION CTA — a yes/no question, not a calendar link
```

---

## Subject Line Formulas

- `quick student question for {{companyName}}`
- `{{senderProject}} — is {{firstName}} the right person?`
- `research help? (student, not a pitch)`

---

## Framework Template

```
SUBJECT: quick student question for {{companyName}}

Hi {{firstName}},

I'm {{senderName}}, a student at {{senderSchool}} working on
{{senderProject}} for {{senderProgram}}. I came across
{{companyName}} while researching {{industry}} and noticed
{{icebreaker}} — that's actually exactly the kind of thing my
project is looking at.

I put together a short {{leadMagnet}} comparing how companies
like yours handle {{painPoint}} — happy to send it over even if
you can't help, just thought it might be useful either way.

Would it be okay if I sent it to you, or pointed you to who on
your team might find it more useful?

{{senderName}}
```

---

## Hormozi Principles Applied

| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Give before you ask** ($100M Leads) | The resource is offered before any request for time or a meeting. |
| **Specificity = believability** (both books) | `{{icebreaker}}` and `{{senderProject}}` make the outreach feel researched, not templated. |
| **Value Equation — low effort ask** | The only ask is a yes/no reply, not a call. |
| **Core Four — cold outreach still needs a value hook** ($100M Leads) | Even a "student" email leans on a real lead magnet, not just a favor-ask. |

## Filled Example

```
SUBJECT: quick student question for Apex Roofing

Hi Mike,

I'm Jordan, a student at UT Austin working on a capstone project
on local service marketing for my Digital Marketing 401 class. I
came across Apex Roofing while researching Austin-area
contractors and noticed you're running a few Google ads right
now — that's actually the exact thing my project is looking at.

I put together a short breakdown of what's working (and what's
not) in local roofing ad campaigns right now — happy to send it
over even if it's not useful, just thought it might help either
way.

Would it be okay if I sent it to you, or is there someone else
on your team it'd make more sense for?

Jordan
```

---

## Tone Checklist

- [ ] Would this sound normal texted to a classmate?
- [ ] No "I hope this email finds you well," no "I wanted to reach out."
- [ ] Contractions used naturally (I'm, that's, don't).
- [ ] One idea per sentence.
- [ ] Value is *offered*, not attached — always ask "want me to send it?"
- [ ] Sign-off is first name only.
- [ ] Under 120 words total.

**Important:** This angle only works if it's true. If it's not an actual school project, it reads as a gimmick and damages trust when discovered.
