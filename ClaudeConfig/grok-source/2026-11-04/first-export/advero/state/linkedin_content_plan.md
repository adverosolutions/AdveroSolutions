## 1. Executive summary

What to do:

1. Post 4 times a week, Tuesday to Friday, and use Monday and the weekend for 15 minutes of real commenting only. Buffer's 2M+ post study and Richard van der Blom's 1.3M post data both land on roughly 2 to 5 posts a week for personal profiles. Your own Phase 1 plan already says 4 a week, so this fits. Drop to 3 (cut Tuesday) if the week gets tight.
2. Lead with native document (PDF carousel) and text plus image posts. Documents lead on reach and engagement in Richard's 2026 report and in Socialinsider's 2026 benchmark (7.00% engagement rate for native documents). Avoid polls and avoid two text-only posts in a row (Richard: about 25% reach loss on the second one).
3. Write long enough to earn it, or very short. Several studies put the best engagement at roughly 1,300 to 2,500 characters. Put a complete hook in the first 140 characters, because that is all most phones show before "see more".
4. Hooks: story and contrarian openers beat question openers in a 309,614 post study (AuthoredUp). Use a story or a flat contrarian claim. Do not open with a generic question.
5. Do NOT use "Comment KEYWORD and I'll send it". LinkedIn is actively filtering pods, automated comments and engagement bait, and a MagicPost study of 684,000 posts shows keyword posts moved from +9% to -52% impressions versus each author's average after the week of July 27, 2026. Put the lead magnet in the post itself (a free native document, no gate), end with a real question, and answer each comment by hand.
6. Proof beats opinion. The posts that convert in this niche are case studies and "here is the workflow" breakdowns. Evidence is anecdotal (self reported posts), so use only real, approved results and use the [PLACEHOLDER] slots until Lorenzo fills them.
7. Be honest about the channel. Evidence that local trades owners (plumbers, roofers, HVAC) are active on LinkedIn is thin. The better use of LinkedIn content for AdveroSolutions is probably (a) proof for the people who click your profile after a connection request, and (b) reaching B2B service owners and peers (commercial cleaning, bookkeeping, recruiting, agency owners). This part is my inference, flagged again in section 7.

## 2. Research findings (with source links)

### 2.1 Formats

- Richard van der Blom's "20 things the data says about LinkedIn in 2026" (June 2026, summary of his Algorithm Insights Report, 1.3M posts): document posts get a 1.39x reach multiplier and are underused; polls have the highest reach multiplier (1.78x) but the lowest engagement multiplier (0.37); text-only posts under 600 characters underperform; two text-only posts back to back cost about 25% reach on the second; lo-fi unscripted video beats polished video by 1.8x to 2.6x; posts without hashtags beat posts with hashtags by 5 to 10%; AI is the number one topic on the platform (1.72x reach multiplier). Fetched. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_20-things-the-data-says-about-linkedin-in-activity-7474027987458260993-JHK1
- Richard van der Blom, Chapter 3 note (formats): documents are the most underused format and outperform everything else on reach and engagement; video fell below average on both for the first time; polls travel far but no longer generate conversation. Same post says LinkedIn rolled out AI moderation aimed at pods, automated comments and engagement bait CTAs ("comment X and I'll send the ebook"), with many posts wrongly removed in the first wave. Fetched. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_59-linkedin-formats-dos-and-donts-chapter-activity-7464297770414133248-T9K2
- Richard van der Blom, March 2026 format scorecard (snippet only): text plus image reach up and highly saveable; text only reach up but engagement down; document or carousel stable, best for light scannable content; polls good for finding buying signals; video reach down; articles and newsletters reach and engagement up. Also says format choice depends on follower tier and posting frequency. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_the-best-format-for-your-linkedin-post-depends-activity-7439941058240737280-10b-
- Creator Science podcast with Richard (snippet only): reach for active creators is down about 60% over two years; best posting frequency has dropped from 5 to 6 a week to 2 to 4; for 3 posts a week he would do text plus image, a carousel, and another text plus image, and skip polls, video and text only; do a newsletter edition at least monthly. https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/
- Socialinsider LinkedIn Benchmarks 2026 (snippet only; 1.3M posts from 16,645 company pages, Jan to Dec 2025): average engagement rate 5.20%; native documents lead at 7.00% (up 14% YoY); video views per post down 36% YoY. Caveat: company pages, not personal profiles. https://cdn.socialinsider.io/documents/linkedin_benchmarks_2026.pdf
- Buffer (best time article, fetched) states carousel posts generate up to 596% more engagement than text-only posts (Buffer's own claim, no method shown in that article). https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/
- Conflict to note: Richard's own posts disagree on video (June: "picking up again"; Chapter 3 note: "below average"; March: reach down). Treat video as a test, not a pillar. Lo-fi, one topic per video, if used.

### 2.2 Cadence

- Buffer, 2M+ posts from 94,000+ accounts (search result): moving from 1 post a week to 2 to 5 is the big step (+1,182 impressions per post); 6 to 10 a week gives more (+5,001); 11+ gives the most (+16,946). Recommends starting at 2 to 5 a week. https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/
- Buffer also says consistency matters more than volume (fetched on the best-time page). https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/
- Compilation of 7 sources (search result, third party): about 2 to 4 posts a week for personal profiles, max one a day, and 10 to 15 minutes a day of commenting when starting out. https://schedulenchill.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin
- LinkedIn Daily freelancer guide (fetched, low authority): 2x a week sustained beats 5x a week for two months then silence. https://linkedindaily.com/linkedin-for-freelancers/

### 2.3 Length and the fold

- AuthoredUp, 372,126 posts (search result snippet): the 1,301 to 2,500 character range had the best median engagement (2.61% to 2.67%) versus 2.10% under 400 characters; fold is about 210 characters desktop and about 140 mobile. https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit
- Ordinal, 248,275 posts (search snippet): highest engagement at 2,000 to 3,000 characters (1.33%) and under 150 characters (1.22%); the 600 to 1,500 range was weakest. https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-post-size
- Taplio (search snippet): 2,000+ characters 2.56% engagement versus 1.53% under 200; text plus image 2.77% versus 1.98% text-only. https://taplio.com/blog/how-long-should-a-linkedin-post-be
- Growtempo, 12,988 posts (search snippet): length itself predicted nothing; the first two lines decided performance. https://www.growtempo.com/blog/linkedin-post-length
- LinkPost, 438,413 posts (search snippet): 1,500+ characters averaged 49% more engagement than under 300; viral hooks paired a pattern interrupt with a specific number in the first 200 characters. https://www.linkpost.gg/en/playbooks/linkedin-algorithm-playbook-2026/study
- Read: the studies disagree on the exact sweet spot, but none says the 600 to 1,500 middle or sub-600 filler wins. Plan: most posts 1,300 to 2,000 characters, hook complete in the first 140.

### 2.4 Hooks

- AuthoredUp, 309,614 personal profile posts (Dec 2025 to May 2026; fetched): median engagement by opener: story 2.60%, contrarian 2.31%, statement 2.27%, results 2.19%, question 2.16% (last). Hooks of 40 characters or fewer had a 2.61% median versus 2.08% for hooks over 200. A closing question added only about 0.07 points. https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-hook-examples
- LinkPost (see above): number plus pattern interrupt in the first 200 characters. https://www.linkpost.gg/en/playbooks/linkedin-algorithm-playbook-2026/study
- Richard's data point #4 and #3 (fetched): reach tracks how abnormally good a post is for you, not how good it is in general. Write for your niche, not for everyone. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_20-things-the-data-says-about-linkedin-in-activity-7474027987458260993-JHK1

### 2.5 Timing

- Sprout Social, 2B engagements across 307,000 profiles (Nov 27, 2025 to Feb 27, 2026; fetched; times in the audience's local time): Monday 1 to 2 PM, Tuesday 11 AM to 5 PM, Wednesday 11 AM to 4 PM, Thursday 11 AM and 1 to 5 PM, Friday 11 AM and 1 to 2 PM; weekends worst. https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-linkedin/
- Buffer, 4.8M posts (published Sep 9, 2026; fetched; normalized to local time): best window 3 to 8 PM on weekdays; top slots Wednesday 4 PM, Friday 3 PM and 4 PM; Monday and Tuesday weakest; morning weaker than afternoon and evening; Thursday best at 5 PM. https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/
- The two disagree (Sprout favors midday and early afternoon, Tuesday to Thursday; Buffer favors late afternoon and evening, Wednesday to Friday). Buffer itself says to treat times as a starting point. The calendar below uses both sources' windows as a built-in A/B test and tells Lorenzo to compare in LinkedIn analytics.

### 2.6 Comments, lead magnets, and what changed in 2026

- MagicPost, 684,000 posts from 16,000 creators (published Sep 30, 2026; fetched): "Comment KEYWORD and I'll send it" posts were +9% impressions before the week of July 27, 2026 and -52% after (62% fewer people reached); comments fell back to about average; 60 of 79 creators using the format saw a drop. Closing questions stayed about 4% below average, link in first comment about 11% below, "DM me" about 26% below. The study says it cannot see why. https://magicpost.in/studies/linkedin-comment-bait
- LinkedIn's own statement on authentic engagement (snippet only): pods and automated comments (browser extension, script or third party tool) are not allowed and may reduce reach or lead to account restrictions. https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations
- LinkedIn on "AI slop" and automation (snippet only): crackdown on comments created at scale with little human involvement, and on responses that only restate the post. https://news.linkedin.com/2026/keeping-conversations-real-on-linkedin
- Richard's data point #16 (fetched): free content generates 20 to 25x more reach than gated content. Also #15: pods detected with about 97% accuracy. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_20-things-the-data-says-about-linkedin-in-activity-7474027987458260993-JHK1
- Links: MagicPost study of 566,957 posts (fetched): attached link cards cut median reach about in half; since mid-May 2026, against the same author's no-link posts, a typed URL is about 21% lower, a link card 19% lower, link in comments 16% lower. https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-external-links-reach (Richard's #8 says 3+ outbound links outperform one link; this conflicts and is unexplained, so keep links out unless the post's job is the click.)
- Comment to DM automation case studies exist but are vendor or self reported: LinkMagnet reports one post with 903 comments and 50 clients (vendor's own founder, 2026), and a sales-tool agency post claims 300 meetings and $7M pipeline from a lead magnet automation. A reply on that second post notes it is easier to do with a B2B sales tool than with services. These were written before or around the July 2026 drop and rely on automation tools that LinkedIn says it filters. Not recommended for a single irreplaceable personal account (your own LinkedIn plan says the same). https://linkmagnet.gg/blog/etude-de-cas-createur-lead-magnet-linkedin and https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dominique-michel-salesautomation_we-turned-viral-linkedin-lead-magnets-into-activity-7495396643190657024-s_n0 (both snippet only)

## 4. Ten hook ideas

Rules used: story or contrarian or flat statement, ideally a complete claim in the first 40 to 140 characters, a specific number only if it is a real number, no generic questions. Any hook that implies a fact about you or a client must be true before it is used. Items marked [TRUE?] need Lorenzo to confirm.

1. "Your slowest employee is your phone." (statement, pillar: AI in home services)
2. "Most contractors do not have a lead problem." (contrarian, pillar: contractor growth)
3. "Stop buying leads. Call the ones you already paid for." (contrarian, ties to reactivation, pillar: lead generation)
4. "AI will not fix a bad follow-up process." (contrarian, pillar: AI in home services)
5. "Nobody wants a chatbot. They want the phone answered." (contrarian, pillar: AI in home services)
6. "Day [N] of my 90 Day Sprint. [One honest number]." [TRUE?] (story, pillar: founder journey)
7. "I built a [workflow name] for a [niche] last week. It has [N] steps and none of them are smart." [TRUE?] (story, pillar: AI in home services)
8. "I almost hung up on a discovery call last week." [TRUE?] (story, pillar: sales)
9. "Speed to lead is a staffing problem, not a marketing problem." (statement, pillar: lead generation)
10. "DFW service business owners: your Google Business Profile is sending you calls you never return." (statement, local angle, only if you are comfortable the claim fits your audience, pillar: contractor growth)

Why these shapes: AuthoredUp (https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-hook-examples) ranks story and contrarian openers above question openers and shows short hooks do better. LinkPost (https://www.linkpost.gg/en/playbooks/linkedin-algorithm-playbook-2026/study) says viral hooks pair a pattern interrupt with a specific number, which is why hooks 6 and 7 leave a slot for a real number.
