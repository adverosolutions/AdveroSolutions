Title: A simple routine for quotes that go quiet

I run an automation consultancy that builds systems for service businesses, so I don't do the cleans myself. The thing I see most is quotes and unfinished bookings that just sit there because nobody owns the next step.

Here's a simple routine you can run with what's already in BookingKoala:

1. Once a day, open your leads and look at anyone who got a quote or started a booking but didn't finish.
2. Put each one in a funnel stage so you can see what's still open and what's lost. [Lorenzo: confirm the exact menu names in BookingKoala]
3. Follow up two or three times at most. [Lorenzo: the timing you'd really recommend, e.g. same day, two days later, a week later]
4. Stop the moment they reply or book. A "still interested?" after someone already booked looks careless.
5. When one is lost, move it to Lost and write down why. After a month you'll see a pattern.

## Draft 2: Agency Service Scaling Kings, General discussion (value post with a lesson)
Why: PPC agency owners, and the same topic got his only multi-reply thread (Julius).

Title: Before you blame the leads, check these first

I build automations for service businesses, mostly around what happens after a lead comes in. I don't run an agency myself.

When a client says the leads are bad, here's what I'd check before touching the campaign:

1. How fast the first reply goes out. Take the last 10 leads and compare the form time with the first call or text.
2. How many never got contacted at all. Ask the client to mark each of those 10 as called, texted or nothing.
3. How many tries they make before giving up.

[Lorenzo, optional: one real thing you found the last time you checked this on a build. Delete if not.]

If those look fine, then it's worth looking at lead quality. If they don't, you have something concrete to bring to the client that isn't "your team is slow."

How do you bring this up with clients without it sounding like blame?
