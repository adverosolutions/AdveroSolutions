# Playbook batch: Offer-first optimization, data-backed benchmarks, 2-email follow-ups, and the "Creative Ideas" + Clay/Claude supply chain

## 1. Frameworks / mental models

**The 4 levers, in order of impact** [9C6WSdOaIrc]
1. **Offer.** Make it easy to say yes to.
2. **List segmentation.** "The list is the message."
3. **Personalization.** A multiplier, never a fix.
4. **Volume.** Last resort, once the first three are dialed in.
- This order assumes deliverability is already healthy. [9C6WSdOaIrc]

**SQL vs MQL offer test** [9C6WSdOaIrc][UDNdgW5cMw0]
- **SQL:** "We solve X. Talk Tuesday?" and they say yes to a meeting.
- **MQL:** a hand-raise for a free asset (report, list, Loom, lead magnet), converted to a meeting later.
- Diagnostic question: *How easy is this to say yes to right now?* If it's hard (rip-and-replace CRM or PM tool, crowded categories like staffing or cyber), test an MQL offer.
- "The biggest shift in success was going from an SQL to an MQL offer." [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- Everyone should test an MQL offer at least in follow-ups. [9C6WSdOaIrc]

**Chunk up / chunk down** [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- **Chunk up:** talk only about results. For example, never say "SEO agency"; say "help law firms increase revenue."
- **Chunk down:** lead with one specific, sexy use case or feature. The Clay example: "find look-alikes of the case study you just closed" instead of "150+ data providers."

**Segmentation principle** [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- Split by shared pain, not just filters. A 2-person agency and a 50-person agency have different problems.
- Other axes: industry, funding, stage, buying signals.
- The win comes from rewriting the message for each smaller segment, not from the split itself.

**Reply rates are relative to TAM and ease-of-yes** [9C6WSdOaIrc][AD2S5rdsmKo]
- A comms-platform overhaul at "1 positive per 2,000" is still viable because the TAM (100+ employee companies) is huge. With a 20k TAM it "would never work." [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- Google review removal (pay only after removal) got "1 positive per 70." At 1/2,000 that offer would be broken. [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- Judge benchmarks against LTV:CAC and TAM. After ~20 experiments, keep running the best one. [AD2S5rdsmKo]

**Why follow-ups fail** [UDNdgW5cMw0][AD2S5rdsmKo]
- They fail because the prospect has no current need, not because they find you annoying.
- Nobody remembers cold emails, so space re-touches months apart and let their situation change.

**Value-prop axes (B2B)** [AD2S5rdsmKo][UDNdgW5cMw0]
- Save time, save money, make money, and possibly reduce risk.
- Health and status are B2C only.

## 2. Copy formulas

**Length**
- Target **70–90 words**. This range was more likely to land in the "excellent" tier than >90 or <70. His hypothesis: very short emails lack credibility and value explanation. [AD2S5rdsmKo]

**Subject lines** [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- Including the **prospect's first name** had the highest correlation with above-average and excellent campaigns. He plans to split-test this "forever" but calls it overused.
- **Mismatched case + under 3 words** also correlated well. His likely pattern: first letter capitalized, the rest lowercase.
  - Template: `{{firstName}} quick idea` / `{{firstName}} hotel vacuums`
- A "little blip" for including the company name. Low confidence.

**First line / personalization** [AD2S5rdsmKo][9C6WSdOaIrc]
- ❌ Kill generic AI openers such as "I saw you sell to fitness coaches and wanted to reach out." Claimed "143% less replies." [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- ✅ Base personalization on signals or triggers (reviews, hires, news), or on a "who they sell to / what they sell" analysis that feeds a *specific idea*. [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- ✅ One AI line mid-email beat a strong human-written control. See the Donut Media claim below. [9C6WSdOaIrc][WlPv4EabCoo]

**Social proof** [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- Don't name the case-study company. Named cases correlated with average or below-average campaigns. Describe the outcome generically instead.
  - ❌ "We do outbound for Reddit.com"
  - ✅ "We helped a {{similar company type}} {{result}}"
- He says he'll "take this to the bank."

**Body: Creative Ideas campaign** (his top performer across clients) [WlPv4EabCoo]
```
Hi {{firstName}}, came across {{company}}. If I were in your shoes, here's how I'd use {{us}}:
• {{idea_1: constrained to offering A}}
• {{idea_2: constrained to offering B}}
• {{idea_3: constrained to offering C}}
{{CTA}}
```
- Short variant: "If I were looking at your business, I'd help by {{AI-generated clause}}." [WlPv4EabCoo]

**Body: Clay chunk-down example** [9C6WSdOaIrc]
> "Saw the work you did with {{case_study_client}} and how you helped them {{result}}. When you close a deal like that, how are you finding other companies that look like them…? Could I show you how it's done sometime on a call?"

**PS line: second offer on a different axis** [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- Claimed "five times more responses."
```
PS: if {{time-saving pitch}} isn't a priority, we also help {{ICP}} {{save money / make money}} by {{mechanism}}.
```

**CTA** [AD2S5rdsmKo][9C6WSdOaIrc]
- ❌ "Do you want to book a meeting?" / "Could we talk?" got way fewer replies.
- ✅ Value-based: "If we could help you with {{X}}, would it be useful?" (credited to Jordan Crawford)
- ✅ Lead magnet / free asset for hand-raises.

**Follow-up recipe** [UDNdgW5cMw0]
- [ ] **Email 1:** SQL ask (meeting), value-prop axis A (e.g., save time).
- [ ] **Email 2 (3–5 days later):** flip the axis (e.g., make money) and **lower the ask** to an MQL or resource offer. Add any context you missed.
- [ ] **Optional Email 3:** final touch or name-drop.
- [ ] **Re-touch after 1–3 months** as a fresh campaign with new insights.

**Name-drop follow-up** [UDNdgW5cMw0]
```
{{firstName}}, is {{improving X}} something {{CEO / Director name}} owns, or is that you?
```
- Replies typically confirm the person, redirect you, or name the right contact.

**"Building it for you" two-step** (from Kyle Rasmussen; "working quite well" as of Feb 24, 2026) [UDNdgW5cMw0]
- **Email 1:** "Hadn't heard of {{company}} before but came across it and thought it was cool. We did {{X}} for {{similar client}}. I'm putting a sample together for you, will send tomorrow."
- **Email 2 (next day):** send the auto-generated asset in the email.
- If they reply before email 2, send the asset immediately.

## 3. Offer / angle patterns

- **Irresistible ask:** Forbes interview offer to event speakers got "one positive response" for "every three people we emailed." [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- **Painful-problem SQL works without a magnet:**
  - Numeral: "Do you want to automate your sales tax collection for your e-commerce brand?" [9C6WSdOaIrc]
  - Creator affiliate matching where you pay only on affiliate basis. [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- **MQL magnets:**
  - GEX's own: "We scraped everyone engaging with your competitors' LinkedIn content, can we send it?" [9C6WSdOaIrc]
  - Staffing: compensation report or market-talent snapshot sent *before* they need to hire. [9C6WSdOaIrc]
  - Vacuums-to-hotels: stop asking "buy now?" and become the next vendor up when timing hits. Positive responses went "way higher." [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- **Performance / risk-reversal:** pay only after the negative review is removed. [9C6WSdOaIrc]
- **Pay-on-results PR:** "80 positive responses per day," best campaign used Creative Ideas. [WlPv4EabCoo]
- **Industries that performed best** (schools, restaurants, RE brokerages, e-com, SaaS) are skewed by specific strong offers. He says don't act on this. [AD2S5rdsmKo]

## 4. List-building & enrichment tactics

**Creative Ideas prompt build in Clay** (claimed <5 min setup) [WlPv4EabCoo]
- [ ] Input column: **company description**. This is the only variable in the user prompt.
- [ ] System prompt, part 1: role, your business context (detailed, not one sentence), "all ideas must be possible with {{channel}}," "infer their ICP from the description."
- [ ] Constrain each bullet to one real offering. Clay's version: idea 1 = new-hire list + target titles, idea 2 = Claygent research, idea 3 = PredictLeads/BuiltWith news or integration.
  - Why: an unconstrained version invented features the client couldn't build.
- [ ] System prompt, part 2: 2 hand-written examples from contrasting business types (he used CPG Red Bull and B2B service Pilot.com).
- [ ] Manually review ~10 target companies and hand-write 3–4 ideal outputs.
- [ ] To fix bad outputs, **add more examples** rather than rewriting instructions.
- [ ] Keep the system prompt heavy and the user prompt minimal to trigger OpenAI **cached-input** discounts.

**Name-drop enrichment** [UDNdgW5cMw0]
- Pull all employees via Blitz API (one call returns names and titles) or Prospeo/Clay.
- Use AI to pick the relevant exec and clean their name.

**Scaled supply chain (Claude Code + Supabase + Clay)** [s2hShfYo7Wo]
1. Build the search in the Prospeo UI, then copy the request JSON.
2. An internal app scrapes the full search via API. Claude Code auto-splits large searches (e.g., 250k).
3. Store in Supabase, indexed by client ID then campaign ID.
4. **Dedupe on LinkedIn URL and email.**
5. Hourly edge-function email waterfall for rows where `enrichment_attempted = false`:
   - **Step 1, cache:** check by LinkedIn slug. If validated <90 days ago, keep it; otherwise re-verify with MillionVerifier.
   - **Step 2, Prospeo finder:** fast, and catch-alls are validated via BounceBan. He trusts these without re-verifying and has seen no bounce increase.
   - **Step 3, Smartlead email-finder API:** slow; based on actual send data and includes LeadMagic/AnyMailFinder validation.
6. Daily at **2 a.m.**, trigger.dev pushes verified leads to each campaign's Clay webhook in batches of **~25 contacts per webhook** (webhooks are workspace-rate-limited).
7. Clay = "final mile": company-description enrichment, title/segment routing, AI copy, Claygent case-study pulls, then into the sequencer.
   - Why Clay over pure Claude Code: visual branching logic, hosted reliability, and debuggability. Vibe-coded last-mile risks sending "crazy emails."

**Forecasting & caching** [s2hShfYo7Wo]
- Expect only "50 to 70%" of pulled contacts to validate, depending on industry. Forecast sendable volume from validated emails, not raw list size.
- Scale thresholds:
  - "Under 5,000 emails a day": use Clay natively.
  - ~500/day: "don't worry about" caching.
  - 1,000–2,000/day: start a DB cache of found emails to cut cost.
- Pricing notes: Prospeo doesn't re-charge for emails already found. Smartlead is "kind of like unlimited pricing." [s2hShfYo7Wo]

## 5. Deliverability / inbox infra notes

- **Sequence length:** a 4th or 5th email increases spam-marking risk and hurts domain reputation. [UDNdgW5cMw0]
- **Spintax:**
  - More than "15 different variables" correlated with worse results. [AD2S5rdsmKo]
  - Yet excellent-tier campaigns ran "five different copy streams." [AD2S5rdsmKo]
  - GEX's SOP is to "hammer it with spin tax" when deliverability drops. Moderate spintax, don't over-spin. [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- **Verification:**
  - Re-verify cached emails older than 90 days. [s2hShfYo7Wo]
  - Keep BounceBan-validated catch-alls. [s2hShfYo7Wo]
- **Reply rate still matters for deliverability,** even though positive-reply math drives decisions. [9C6WSdOaIrc]

**Sequence length by TAM** ("really rough numbers") [UDNdgW5cMw0]
- ~1M contacts: 1 email, then re-hit the whole TAM with new insights.
- 50k–100k: 2 emails.
- <50k: 3 emails.
- Default recommendation is 2, maximum 3. [AD2S5rdsmKo][UDNdgW5cMw0]

**Spacing**
- 3–5 days between steps; "doesn't matter at all" exactly how many. [UDNdgW5cMw0]
- Rest 1–3 months before re-touching, stated elsewhere as ~2 months. [UDNdgW5cMw0][AD2S5rdsmKo]

**Send days** [AD2S5rdsmKo]
- Mon–Thu are best, Tue–Thu even better.
- Weekends aren't worth it for B2B.
- He's considering stopping after ~1 p.m. Friday.
- For small TAMs, send Tue–Thu only.

**Iterate, then scale** [9C6WSdOaIrc]
