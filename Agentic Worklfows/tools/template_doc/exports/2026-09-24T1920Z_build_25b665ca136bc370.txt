Every email the system sends
Every email the system sends, in the order a lead meets them.
How to use it: Change any words you like. Keep the words in {curly brackets}: they are filled in for each lead. When you are done, tell Claude 'done'. Nothing goes live until you say so.
When you say 'done', everything in this doc counts as approved. Emails still only go out after you tell Claude 'go live'.
Approved does not mean every email goes out on its own. First replies never do: the system drafts them and you send them yourself from the email thread. The other replies to a lead go out on their own only after a reply of yours has reached that lead, except the Locked in booking confirmation. Every send check still applies.
If a sentence appears in many emails (like the time-offer line in first replies), changing it once is enough; it changes everywhere. If you change two copies differently, Claude will ask.
Leave the small grey tags at the end of each heading as they are: they tell Claude which email is which. Grey lines marked note or section are only there to help you read.
Type your changes straight into the text: no suggesting mode, no strikethrough, no highlights or text colours. Claude reads only the words, so a doc with any of those is not approved; Claude asks you instead.
Other versions of an email, like 'If there are no free times:', show what they change: a grey line quotes the lines of the email above that they replace or leave out, and the lines under it are the new ones. The versions with example dates and times are written out in full.
Words in brackets: {first_name} their first name, {company} their company, {slots} two open times, {slot} one time, {zoom_link} the call link, {loom_url} your Loom video, {original_subject} the subject of their email. The cold emails use Instantly's double brackets, like {{firstName}}.
If we don't have their first name, 'Hey {first_name},' becomes 'Hey there,'. If we don't have their company name, {company} becomes 'your business'. A follow-up email that names the company is held until we have it. In a referral reply with no name, {referral_name} becomes 'the right person' and {referral_first_name} becomes 'them'.
How times are written
Every time is Central (CT). The engine writes them like this:
{slots}: Tue 12:05pm or Thu 4:30pm CT (just Tue 12:05pm CT when only one time is free)
{slot}: Tue 12:05pm (the email adds CT after it)
{slot1} and {slot2}: Tue 12:05pm CT
{when_ct}: Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT, and in another timezone Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT (1:05pm ET)
{time_ct}: 12:05pm CT
{asked_time}: the time they asked for, in their own zone, like Mon 10:45am ET
{lead_local_time}: their own time, like 1:05pm ET (only added when they are not on Central)
{alt_slots}: like {slots}; when the times are in another week the dates are added, like Tue Jan 15 12:05pm or Thu Jan 17 4:30pm CT
Zones are written CT, ET, MT, PT, and MST for Arizona.
Exactly how the engine writes times, from its own code (part of every email with a time in it): (tag: formats.1)
Example times: Mon Jan 7 9:45, Tue Jan 8 12:05 and Thu Jan 10 16:30, 2030, Central (America/Chicago). This block is the same on every template that has a time or slot in it.
Two times, {slots} and {alt_slots}: Tue 12:05pm or Thu 4:30pm CT
One time, {slots}, {alt_slots}, {slot1} and {slot2}: Tue 12:05pm CT
A time without its zone, {slot}: Tue 12:05pm | Thu 4:30pm | Mon 9:45am
A counter-proposal that crosses a week, {alt_slots}: Tue Jan 15 12:05pm or Thu Jan 17 4:30pm CT
The time the lead proposed, in the zone they wrote it in, {asked_time}: America/Chicago = Mon 9:45am CT | America/New_York = Mon 10:45am ET | America/Denver = Mon 8:45am MT | America/Los_Angeles = Mon 7:45am PT
The time the lead proposed, when the counter-proposal crosses a week, {asked_time}: America/Chicago = Mon Jan 7 9:45am CT | America/New_York = Mon Jan 7 10:45am ET | America/Denver = Mon Jan 7 8:45am MT | America/Los_Angeles = Mon Jan 7 7:45am PT
The lead's own time after the call time, {lead_local_time}: America/Chicago = (nothing added) | America/New_York = (1:05pm ET your time) | America/Denver = (11:05am MT your time) | America/Los_Angeles = (10:05am PT your time) | America/Phoenix = (11:05am MST your time)
The call time, {when_ct}: Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT
The call time when the lead's timezone is known, {when_ct}: America/Chicago = Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT | America/New_York = Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT (1:05pm ET) | America/Denver = Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT (11:05am MT) | America/Los_Angeles = Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT (10:05am PT) | America/Phoenix = Tue, Jan 8 at 12:05pm CT (11:05am MST)
The clock time only, {time_ct}: 12:05pm CT | 4:30pm CT
