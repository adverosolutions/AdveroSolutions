FREE VALUE DELIVERABLE
What to Build, How to Deliver, and Why It Works
Internal Team Guide

This document defines the free value asset that gets sent when a prospect replies "yes" to Outreach 1.

================================================================================

1. WHAT THE EMAIL PROMISES

The email tells the prospect:

"Happy to put together a visual breakdown for {{companyName}} showing what that looks like from first inquiry to estimate booked, without it taking up your team's time or needing to bring someone on to manage it. Takes about a minute to look at."

This means when someone replies "yeah send it over," we need to deliver exactly that. A visual that shows the journey from inquiry to booked estimate or appointment, with the scheduling and follow-up handled automatically. One page. Fast to digest. Clear enough that the owner looks at it and either thinks "we're already doing this" or "we're definitely not doing this."

RULE: The deliverable must match the promise. Nothing more, nothing less. If the email says "visual breakdown" and "one minute to review," that's what they get.

================================================================================

2. THE THREE JOBS THE FREE VALUE HAS TO DO

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
JOB 1: SHOW THEM THE TIMELINE THEY'RE NOT SEEING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Most roofing and HVAC owners have never actually mapped out what happens between a lead reaching out and an estimate landing on the calendar. They know it happens. They don't know what it looks like laid out.

The visual should show time moving left to right. Homeowner inquires on the left. Estimate booked on the right. Everything in between is the gap your system fills.

The key moments to show on the timeline:

MINUTE 0:
Homeowner submits inquiry (form, call, text).

WITHIN MINUTES:
Instant acknowledgment goes out. The homeowner knows someone received their request and a response is coming.

HOUR 1-2:
Follow-up if no response to the first touch. Keeps the conversation alive while the homeowner is still in decision mode.

HOUR 6-12:
Second follow-up with scheduling prompt. Moves the conversation toward getting an estimate on the calendar.

DAY 1-2:
Reminder if still no response. Catches the homeowners who got busy and forgot to reply.

DAY 3+:
Final check-in before the lead goes cold. Last touch before the window closes.

AT ANY POINT:
When the homeowner responds, the booking flow triggers and the estimate gets on the calendar. No waiting for someone on the team to see it and schedule manually.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
JOB 2: SHOW THEM THE GAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Somewhere on this visual, the owner needs to see where things typically break down without a system. Not by accusing them. By showing what the timeline looks like when it depends on someone being available versus when it runs on its own.

The simplest way to do this is a side-by-side or a before/after.

LEFT SIDE: "WHEN IT DEPENDS ON YOUR TEAM"
Gaps between steps. Delays. Question marks where follow-ups should be. Leads going quiet because nobody had time to check. The homeowner moves on to the next company.

RIGHT SIDE: "WHEN IT RUNS ON ITS OWN"
Clean, consistent, no gaps. Every step happens on time regardless of how busy the day is. The homeowner stays engaged from first inquiry to booked estimate.

You're not telling them their process is broken. You're showing two versions and letting them see which one looks more like what they're running now. They draw their own conclusion.

RULE: The gap is shown, never stated. You never say "your process has gaps." You show two timelines and let them compare.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
JOB 3: MAKE THEM ASK "HOW DOES THIS ACTUALLY WORK?"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The visual should show WHAT happens at each step, not HOW it happens.

It shows "instant acknowledgment goes out" but it doesn't explain what technology sends it. It shows "follow-up goes out at hour 2" but it doesn't explain the automation behind it. It shows "estimate gets booked on the calendar" but it doesn't show the booking system.

This is critical. The free value creates a gap between "I can see the outcome" and "I don't know how this would actually run." That gap is what earns you the next conversation.

When they reply with "this looks good, how would this work for us?" or "what would it take to set this up?" that's your earned right to explain the system, get on a call, and start the real sales conversation.

RULE: Show the WHAT. Never show the HOW. The HOW is what the sales conversation is for.

================================================================================

3. WHAT IT NEEDS TO LOOK LIKE

FORMAT:
One page. Not a deck. Not a PDF with 5 pages. Not a video. One visual they can look at on their phone in under 60 seconds.

The format should be either a clean flowchart or a horizontal timeline. Something that reads left to right or top to bottom with clear steps. It should look professional but not overdesigned.

KEY: If it looks like a marketing agency made it, they'll treat it like a brochure. If it looks like an operator mapped it out to show them something specific, they'll engage with it.

PERSONALIZATION:
Use their company name on it. "{{companyName}} Inquiry Response Flow" at the top. This is the single biggest signal that you built this for them, not grabbed it from a template folder.

If you can, tailor the homeowner scenario to their niche. For roofing: "Homeowner requests estimate after storm damage." For HVAC: "Homeowner requests service after AC goes out." Small detail, big impact.

VISUAL STYLE:
Clean and simple. Minimal colors. Easy to read on a phone screen. No stock photos, no icons that look like clip art, no gradients or shadows that scream "marketing collateral."

Think of how a good operator would sketch something on a whiteboard to explain it to a colleague. That's the energy. Professional enough to forward to a partner, simple enough to understand in one look.

LENGTH:
The email promises "takes about a minute to look at." The visual must deliver on that. If it takes more than 60 seconds to understand, it's too complex. Strip it down until someone can glance at it and get the point.

================================================================================

4. WHAT IT CANNOT BE

The free value is the first real deliverable the prospect sees from you. If it feels like a sales tool, you lose the trust the email sequence built. Everything below is a hard rule.

NEVER: A PITCH DECK OR SALES PRESENTATION
No slides about your company, your team, your services, your process, or your results. The visual is about their business, not yours.

NEVER: AN EXPLANATION OF AI, AUTOMATION, OR TECHNOLOGY
The visual shows outcomes, not tools. "Follow-up goes out at hour 2" is an outcome. "Our AI-powered automation system triggers a follow-up sequence" is a pitch. Show the what, never the how.

NEVER: PRICING, PACKAGES, OR SERVICE TIERS
Nothing about what it costs or what's included. The free value is free. The pricing conversation comes later, after they've asked for it.

NEVER: A CALL-TO-ACTION LIKE "BOOK A CALL" OR "SCHEDULE A DEMO"
The visual doesn't ask them to do anything except look at it. The reply you send with it asks "let me know what stands out to you." That's the next micro-yes. No calendar links, no booking pages, no demo requests.

NEVER: SOMETHING THAT LOOKS PRE-BUILT FOR EVERYONE
Their company name must be on it. If it looks like a generic template you send to every prospect, it defeats the purpose of the personalized email that earned the reply.

NEVER: MORE THAN ONE PAGE
One page. One visual. If you can't show it in one page, you're showing too much. Strip it down to the essential timeline.

NEVER: SOMETHING THAT REQUIRES A LOGIN, DOWNLOAD, OR CLICK-THROUGH
Attach it directly in the reply. No links to portals, no "check your inbox for access," no file-sharing platforms that require an account. The visual goes directly in the email or as a direct attachment.

================================================================================

5. HOW TO DELIVER IT

THE REPLY:
When a prospect replies yes to the micro-yes, respond with something like:

"Here's the breakdown for {{companyName}}. It maps out the full window from first inquiry to estimate booked. Take a look and let me know what stands out to you."

Attach the visual directly. Image or single-page PDF. No links, no portals.

WHY "WHAT STANDS OUT TO YOU" WORKS:
"Let me know what stands out to you" is the next micro-yes in the sequence. You're not asking them to buy anything. You're not asking them to get on a call. You're asking them to react.

Whatever they react to becomes your opening into the real conversation:

IF THEY SAY: "We're already doing most of this"
Your response: "That's great. Most companies I talk to have the first response covered but the follow-up and reminders are where things tend to fall off when it gets busy. Is that part running on its own for you too?"

IF THEY SAY: "We definitely don't have this"
Your response: "That's actually pretty common. Most companies I work with were in the same spot. Want me to walk you through what it would look like to get this running for {{companyName}}?"

IF THEY SAY: "How would this actually work?"
This is the golden response. They're asking you to sell them. Your response: "Happy to walk you through it. Want to jump on a quick call this week? I can show you exactly how it would plug into what you're already running."

IF THEY SAY: "Looks interesting" OR "Thanks for sending"
Lukewarm response. Your reply: "Of course. Out of curiosity, is the follow-up piece something that's running on its own for you right now, or is that still depending on the team?" This pulls them back into the conversation by asking a specific question tied to the visual.

TIMING:
Respond within 2-4 hours of their reply during business hours. Fast enough to show you're engaged, not so fast that it feels automated. If they reply after hours, respond first thing the next morning.

RULE: The visual should feel like you built it after they said yes, even if the template was ready. The speed of your reply should support that perception. Too fast feels pre-built. Too slow feels like you forgot about them.

================================================================================

6. PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

Here's how to build and deliver the free value at scale without it becoming a bottleneck.

STEP 1: BUILD THE MASTER TEMPLATE
Create one clean visual template that has all the timeline steps, the side-by-side comparison, and placeholder text for the company name and niche scenario. This is your base.

STEP 2: CUSTOMIZE PER LEAD
When a prospect replies yes, duplicate the template and make three changes:
1. Add their company name to the title ("{{companyName}} Inquiry Response Flow").
2. Adjust the homeowner scenario to their niche (storm damage for roofing, AC failure for HVAC).
3. If you know anything specific about their operation from the personalization research (24/7 services, large service area, etc.), reflect that in the timeline. For example, if they offer 24/7 emergency services, the timeline should show after-hours inquiries being handled.

STEP 3: EXPORT AND ATTACH
Export as a single-page PDF or high-quality image. Attach directly in your reply. No links.

STEP 4: SEND THE REPLY
Use the reply template from Section 5. Keep it short. Attach the visual. Ask "let me know what stands out to you."

TIME PER LEAD:
With the master template built, customization should take 2-5 minutes per lead. If it's taking longer than that, the template needs simplifying.

GOAL: The free value should feel custom but be operationally scalable. 80% template, 20% personalization. The company name and niche scenario are the 20% that makes it feel built for them.

================================================================================

7. QUICK REFERENCE SUMMARY

WHAT IT IS:
A one-page visual breakdown showing the full timeline from homeowner inquiry to booked estimate/appointment, with scheduling and follow-up running automatically.

WHAT IT DOES:
Job 1: Shows them the timeline they've never mapped out. Job 2: Shows the gap between team-dependent and automated. Job 3: Makes them ask "how does this actually work?"

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE:
One page. Flowchart or horizontal timeline. Their company name on it. Clean, professional, not overdesigned. Readable on a phone in under 60 seconds.

WHAT IT CANNOT BE:
Not a pitch deck. Not a technology explanation. Not a pricing sheet. Not a pre-built template that looks generic. Not more than one page. Not behind a login or link.

HOW TO DELIVER IT:
Reply within 2-4 hours. Attach directly. Say: "Here's the breakdown for {{companyName}}. Take a look and let me know what stands out to you."

WHAT COMES NEXT:
Their reaction to the visual opens the real sales conversation. Whatever they say, you have a specific response that moves the conversation forward without pitching.
