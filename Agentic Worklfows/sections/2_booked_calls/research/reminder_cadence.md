# Reminder cadence for booked discovery calls

Research note, 2026-09-23. Question: which reminder cadence cuts no-shows for a 15-minute Google Meet call booked by a cold B2B lead (small US service-firm owner)?

**Evidence caveat.** The best causal evidence (randomized trials) comes from healthcare appointments. B2B sales data is mostly vendor self-reports without control groups or sample sizes. Where a healthcare result is applied to Lorenzo's calls, that is marked as extrapolation.

## 1. Bottom line

- **The biggest lever comes before any reminder: offer the nearest slots first.** In the most-cited lead-time data, show rate drops as the call gets further away (80.8% for day-1 bookings, about 75% for days 2 to 4, about 60% by day 14; 6,414 meetings, second-hand) [14]. A 105-study review found long lead time is one of the two most common predictors of a no-show [13]. By default the two offered times should fall within the next 1 to 2 business days, and only go out to 5 days when needed.
- **Two or three reminders beat one, and there is no evidence for more than three.** Randomized trials show a second reminder lowers no-shows [3][4], and multiple reminders beat a single one in the meta-analysis [1]. So a confirmed lead gets three automated moments (booking, day before or morning of, 15 minutes before). Extra touches go only to leads who have not confirmed.
- **Recommended cadence (with SMS):**
  1. **At booking:** email from Lorenzo that confirms the time in the lead's time zone, gives the Meet link and 2 or 3 lines on what the call covers, and asks for a typed reply ("Will you be at a desk or on the road? Either works, Meet runs fine on a phone. And what's the best cell if I'm running late?"). Send the calendar invite with their problem in the title. SMS "booked" note only if a number is on file.
  2. **About 48 hours before, only if booked 3 or more days out:** the pre-read email (moved from +15 minutes).
  3. **24 hours before** (or **08:30 their time on the call day** if booked less than about 20 hours out): email and SMS, "Still good for Thu 2:00 CT? A quick yes works."
  4. **4 hours before, if there is still no reply:** Lorenzo calls, or sends a short personal note he writes himself.
  5. **1 hour before:** SMS, **only to leads who have not confirmed**.
  6. **15 minutes before:** SMS to everyone with the Meet link and "reply if running late".
  7. **3 minutes after start, if they have not joined:** "I'm on, here's the link, or want me to just call you?"
- **Email-only variant (until A2P is approved):** the same steps, sent as short replies in the original email thread. Replace the 1-hour and 15-minute SMS with one email about 60 minutes before, carrying the link and "reply if 2:00 no longer works". At step 4, call when a phone number is on file. Campaign approval can take hours to days, and longer after a rejection [25].
- **By lead time:** *same day* (4 to 8 hours out): booking email, the 15-minute touch, and the 1-hour touch only if the booking email got no reply. Drop the 4-hour task, since it would fire at booking. *Next day:* booking email, morning-of confirm, 15-minute touch, plus the 4-hour and 1-hour touches if unconfirmed. *2 to 5 days:* the full list.
- **Confirm asks:** word them as a natural question, not "reply C". Count any reply as a confirmation. Never treat silence as a cancellation; silence is the signal to escalate to a human touch.
- **Cut:** the separate +15-minute pre-read for short lead times, "Reply C" wording, and one-click cancel links. Offer "reply with a better time" instead.

## 3. Findings by question

### Timing
- **Healthcare trials.** A trial of 54,066 patients compared a reminder 3 days before, 1 day before, or both. Missed-visit rates were 5.8%, 5.3% and 4.4%; in high-risk patients, 25.0%, 24.2% and 20.5%. Cancellations and satisfaction did not change [3]. A second trial covered 125,076 primary-care visits: a second text cut no-shows by 7% (RR 0.93) [4]. A meta-analysis found no clear difference by timing, but multiple notifications beat single ones (RR 1.49 vs 1.09 for attendance) [1]. (Extrapolation: patients, not business owners.)
- **B2B practice disagrees on how many.** Chili Piper: 24 hours, 1 hour, 1 minute, skipping the 24-hour one inside 24 hours [15]. Calendly: 24 hours plus 4 hours [17] or 30 minutes [18]. Will Aitken (Chili Piper) only sends day-before and day-of [16]. SalesHive: 4 touches including 48 hours [21].
- **48h** is worth it only for bookings 3 or more days out, where show rates start to fall [3][14]. **5 minutes before:** no evidence, and it overlaps the 15-minute touch.
- **Time of day:** Gong reports 30% better show odds for a 4 pm call than an 8 am one (no sample size given) [23]. That supports the current 12:00 to 18:00 CT window.

### Channel
- In a Cochrane review (8 trials, 6,615 people), text reminders raised attendance from 67.8% to 78.6% (RR 1.14). Phone calls reached 80.3% [2]. Every study in the digital meta-analysis used SMS, so **there is almost no controlled evidence for email-only reminders** [1].
- Reminders from staff beat automated ones: no-shows were 13.6% with staff reminders, 17.3% automated and 23.1% with none (n=9,835) [5]. Live calls to high-risk patients cut no-shows from 36.2% to 32.8% (n=5,840) [6]. This backs making the 4-hour task a personal call.
- The "98% SMS open rate" figure has no traceable primary source. Treat it as **unverified** [24].
- Email-only: email-heavy vendor cadences report good results (Chili Piper claims under 5% outbound no-shows [16]; Calendly users report about 28% fewer, self-reported, no sample size [17]). No source isolates email, so expect it to do somewhat worse than email plus SMS (**opinion**).

### Confirm-by-reply
- **Commitment works; the evidence is indirect.** Patients writing their own appointment card cut missed NHS appointments 18%; only repeating it aloud cut 3.5% (before/after, not randomized) [10]. A prompt to write down a date *and time* raised flu-shot uptake 4.2 points over 33.1% (n=3,272); a date alone did not [11]. A restaurant asking "Will you call?" and waiting for a yes reportedly cut no-shows from 30% to 10% (anecdote) [12].
- **Non-responders are poorly measured.** In a small observational study, no-shows were 3% when reached live, 24% after voicemail, 39% when not reached (n=250) [7]. Partly selection, but silence marks the at-risk group. Nothing supports treating silence as a cancellation.
- **Specific wording beats generic.** A specific cost message cut missed visits from 11.1% to 8.4%; a general version did less (9.9% vs 8.2%; about 10,000 per trial) [8]. In an A/B test of 161,587 patients, informational wording did least well [9].

### Other tactics
- **Calendar acceptance:** practitioners call a live accept the cheapest commitment [14], and suggest retitling an unaccepted invite "(Please Confirm:)" [16]. No data found. **Opinion.**
- **Pre-read / agenda:** every B2B source says to remind people why they booked and what they get [14][15][22]. Josh Braun suggests the subject "{Confirmed} Tuesday at 3 p.m." and a low-pressure line [22]. No controlled data.
- **Personal video:** Vidyard says an auto-sent confirmation video cut its no-shows from 13% to 9% in a month. That is a vendor self-report with no control group or sample size [19]. **Weak.**

### What to cut
- In one trial, a second reminder did not lower satisfaction [3]. The claim that opt-outs rise with message volume is **unverified**. No source supports more than 3 or 4 touches.
- Reminders raise cancellations [5][9]. That is better than a no-show, because the slot can be rebooked. Chili Piper puts a reschedule link in every reminder [15]; offer "reply with a better time", not a one-click cancel (**opinion**).

### Cold-outbound specifics
- Benchmarks vary widely. Chili Piper claims 95% show on outbound [15]. A newsletter quotes Chili Piper internal data at 80 to 90% show across about 200 outbound meetings a month, and cites Gong figures of 56.9% (average rep) and 72.5% (top quartile) for meetings set on cold calls; the Gong figures are **unverified** at the source [14]. SalesHive gives 25 to 35% no-shows for cold outbound, unsourced [21]. **Plan on 60 to 80% show until our own data exists.**
- By industry: inbound B2B no-shows run 6.5% overall but 15.1% in real estate and 18.1% in education (6,428 meetings) [20]. Buyers closer to small businesses sit at the high end.
- Owners on the road: make it easy to join from a phone, and offer a call as the fallback. **Opinion.**

## 4. Open risks / what we could test

- **Volume.** Detecting a 70% vs 80% show rate needs about 290 calls per arm (80% power). Until then, log every call with: lead-time bucket, confirmed or not, booking path (picked by email reply or via link), vertical, and whether a phone number exists. Review the numbers monthly.
- **SMS consent.** Numbers scraped onto lead records were not given for texting. Asking for the best cell in the booking email creates consent and a commitment at once. Check A2P and TCPA rules before texting a number the lead did not give.
- **Wording risk.** An SMS-style "reply C" can read as automated and undercut the peer voice. Test the natural question against it.
- **Time zones.** Always show the lead's local time next to CT.
- **Tests once volume allows:** a mid-gap touch at 48 hours (on vs off); the 1-hour SMS to everyone vs unconfirmed only; a 30-second Loom in the confirmation email; nearest-slot offers vs spread-out offers.
