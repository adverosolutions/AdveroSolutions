# R4: one-hour heads-up with the call link easy to tap, and an easy out if the time no longer works
Timing: 1 hour before the call, to everyone  Channel: email

## Version A
Subject: our call at {time}
Hey {first_name},

Here's the link for our call in about an hour:
{call_link}

If {time} no longer works, just reply and we'll find another time.

Lorenzo

## Version A, no call link (only if the message shows {call_link})
Hey {first_name},

Our call is in about an hour. The Google Meet link is on your calendar invite.

If {time} no longer works, just reply and we'll find another time.

Lorenzo

## Version B
Subject: see you at {time}
Hey {first_name},

Talk in an hour. Here's the link so you don't have to dig for the invite:
{call_link}

If {time} doesn't work anymore, just reply and we'll move it.

Lorenzo

## Version B, no call link (only if the message shows {call_link})
Hey {first_name},

Talk in an hour. The Google Meet link is on the calendar invite from when we booked.

If {time} doesn't work anymore, just reply and we'll move it.

Lorenzo

## Version C
Subject: talk at {time}
Hey {first_name},

Heads up, we're about an hour out. Easiest way in is this link:
{call_link}

Something come up? Just reply and I'll move us to another time.

Lorenzo

## Version C, no call link (only if the message shows {call_link})
Hey {first_name},

Heads up, we're about an hour out. The Google Meet link is on your calendar invite.

Something come up? Just reply and I'll move us to another time.

Lorenzo

## Pick
B because "Talk in an hour" reads like a text from Lorenzo, and "so you don't have to dig for the invite" gives the email a human reason to exist.
The link sits alone on its own line for a clean tap, and the out names {time} so moving it takes one reply.

## Notes for Lorenzo (optional, max 3 short lines)
Every subject works for both the link and no-link variants, so the no-link versions need no subject of their own.
The opener says "in an hour" instead of the clock time, so it reads right in any time zone; {time} appears only in the out (C leaves it to the subject).
None of the versions uses "still good", "today at" or a confirm ask, so R4 doesn't echo R2 or R3 and reads fine for leads who already said yes.
