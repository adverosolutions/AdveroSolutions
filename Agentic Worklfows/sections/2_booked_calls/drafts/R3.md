# R3: morning-of check that today's call still works, with the link at hand and an easy way to move it
Timing: 8:30am the lead's local time on the call day, only when they booked after the 24-hour mark  Channel: email

## Version A
Subject: today at {time}
Hey {first_name},

Does today at {time} still work on your end?

Here's the link so it's handy: {call_link}

If today got busy, just reply with a better time or grab one here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version A, no call link
Hey {first_name},

Does today at {time} still work on your end?

The link is on the calendar invite whenever you need it.

If today got busy, just reply with a better time or grab one here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version B
Subject: our call today
Hey {first_name},

Talking at {time} today, right? Link for when it's time: {call_link}

If your day filled up, no problem. Reply with a time that works better, or pick one here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version B, no call link
Hey {first_name},

Talking at {time} today, right? The link is on your calendar invite.

If your day filled up, no problem. Reply with a time that works better, or pick one here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version C
Subject: quick check for today
Hey {first_name},

Morning. Are we still set for {time} today? Link's here: {call_link}

If something came up, reply with a better time or grab one at {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version C, no call link
Hey {first_name},

Morning. Are we still set for {time} today? The link's on your calendar invite.

If something came up, reply with a better time or grab one at {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Pick
C because it is the shortest (28 words) for someone who just got R1, and "Morning." fits an 8:30am send and reads like a person.
The yes/no question means any reply counts as a yes, and the out is one line.

## Notes for Lorenzo (optional, max 3 short lines)
R4's spec calls it the only same-day email with the link; if that should hold, always send R3's no-link variant.
{time} shows CT only, so a lead in another zone converts it on the day; consider adding their local time in brackets as {when} does.
C's "Morning." assumes the 8:30am send; if a send can slip past noon their time, use A.
