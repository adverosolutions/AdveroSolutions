# Shared brief: booked-call reminders and no-show recovery (section 2, DRAFT 2026-09-23)

Status: reminder set (R1 to R5) ready for writers, 2026-09-23; the no-show set is added after Lorenzo signs off on its cadence. Research: `research/`. Model: `../1_email_followup/writer_brief.md`.

You are one of several writers. Each writer drafts ONE message of this sequence. A final editor ties them together. Write for Lorenzo Villarreal, founder of AdveroSolutions.

## The situation every message is written for
1. The lead got a cold email, replied that they are interested, and booked a 15-minute call with Lorenzo (they picked one of two times he offered, or used his booking link).
2. They already have: a short "Locked in for {when}" reply from Lorenzo in the original email thread, and a calendar invite with the Google Meet link.
3. These messages come from Lorenzo's own email address (a new email, so you write a subject) or, once texting is enabled, as a text from his business number (a number they have not saved, so every text says it is Lorenzo).
4. Reminders: goal is that they show up, on time, with the link at hand. If the time no longer works, the goal is a new time, not a silent no-show.
5. No-show recovery: they did not join. Goal is a new time on the calendar. Assume something came up; never guilt.
6. One ask per message.

## The sequence (Lorenzo picked this on 2026-09-23; follow it)
Email only for now. Texts come later, once his texting number is registered; do not write texts.

What the lead already has before any of these: Lorenzo's reply in the email thread, "Hey {first_name}, Locked in {when}. The calendar invite with the video link follows in a separate email. I will keep it to 15 minutes. Lorenzo" (or, if they booked through the link, a similar confirmation with three lines on what the call covers). That confirmation stays as it is.

Where they land (Lorenzo, 2026-09-23): every email goes as a reply in the original cold-email thread, from the same inbox the lead has been talking to, so it shows as "Re: <their original subject>". Only when a lead has no thread does it go as a new email, and only then is your subject used. So write a subject, but the body must make sense without it.

- **R1, pre-read.** Timing: 15 minutes after booking when the call is 0 to 2 days away; about 48 hours before the call when it is 3 or more days away. Today it is a one-page note per offer (what the system does, what it costs, what Lorenzo needs from them, one proof line, the three questions he will ask). The body sections stay as they are. Your job is the part around them: a subject, an opening of one or two short lines that works at both timings, and a closing ask that gets a typed reply: the best cell number to reach them if either of them is running late. That ask also gives Lorenzo a number they chose to give him.
- **R2, day-before check.** Timing: 24 hours before the call. A plain question, "still good for {when}?", with the call link at hand and an easy out if the time no longer works (reply with a better time, or {booking_url}). Any reply counts as a yes. Today's version (being replaced, it reads automated): "We are on for tomorrow, {when}. Reply C to confirm and I will have everything ready for {company}. If the time no longer works, grab a new one here instead: {booking_url}". Do not use "Reply C" or any code word.
- **R3, morning-of check.** Timing: 8:30am their time on the day of the call, only when they booked after the 24-hour mark (for example, booked yesterday afternoon for today at 2pm). Same job as R2, worded for "today at {time}". They got R1 recently, so keep it very short.
- **R4, one-hour heads-up.** Timing: 1 hour before the call, to everyone. It is the only same-day email with the link, so the link must be easy to tap. An easy out: reply if {time} no longer works. It must not repeat R2 or R3's wording.
- **R5, Lorenzo's personal touch (internal, not sent by the system).** Timing: 4 hours before the call, only if the lead has not replied to anything since booking. The system gives Lorenzo a task; he calls from his own phone and, if there is no answer, leaves a voicemail and sends a text by hand. Write: the voicemail (15 seconds when spoken, about 35 words) and the text he sends after it (one or two sentences). Both sound like him, mention the call time, and make it easy to move the call. Today's version: text "Hey {first_name}, Lorenzo here. Still good for {time} today? Call link: {call_link}".

## The no-show sequence (Lorenzo picked this on 2026-09-23; follow it)
The lead did not join the call. Times below are from the call's scheduled start (S). Email only for now, all in the same thread as above. Any reply from the lead stops the sequence (Lorenzo or the engine answers it). If they never rebook, they move to a separate long-term nurture after the last message. Offer exactly two times ({slots}) wherever times are offered; the booking link is the fallback.

- **N1, "I'm on" nudge.** S+3 minutes, they have not joined. Lorenzo is sitting in the call. "I'm on, here's the link, or want me to just call you?" Very short, warm, zero blame; they may just be running late. Include {call_link} (and a no-link variant).
- **N2, Lorenzo's call (internal, not sent by the system).** S+5 to S+8: Lorenzo calls from his own phone. Write the 15-second voicemail (about 35 words; says "Lorenzo Villarreal" once; says he will send new times by email in the same thread) and the text he sends by hand after it (one or two sentences).
- **N3, reschedule.** About S+25, once the call is clearly missed. No blame, assume something came up. Two new times ({slots}), soonest first, "reply with the one that works", or {booking_url}. Also write the **second-no-show variant**: this is the second call they have missed; offer only the link (they pick the time) and a plain "no problem if now is a bad time". No guilt in either.
- **N4, next business day.** Two lines. The times again (the system refreshes {slots} if the old ones are gone). Must not repeat N3's wording.
- **N5, day 3 or 4, one useful thing.** One specific point about how the system works for their kind of business, taken only from the offers table and proof list below (no invented facts or numbers), then the same soft ask with {slots}. Write the email once with a {niche_point} slot and then one {niche_point} line per offer (six lines).
- **N6, day 7, one question with three easy answers.** "Still want to chat, later (when?), or not a fit?" Any of the three is a fine answer. Keep {booking_url} for "still want to chat". This is also sent, unchanged, on day 3 or 4 after a second no-show, so do not refer to a specific number of missed calls.
- **N7, day 14, close the loop.** No ask beyond "the link still works" ({booking_url}). No guilt, no "I've tried many times". After this they go to the long-term nurture.
- **N8, after a third missed call.** One or two lines, no guilt, no times offered: Lorenzo will not chase, and they can reply any time if things change.

## Placeholders you may use (and only these)
- {first_name}: their first name. If unknown the system writes "there".
- {company}: their company name (falls back to "your business").
- {when}: day, date and time, rendered like "Tue, Sep 29 at 2:00pm CT", with their local time in brackets when known, e.g. "Tue, Sep 29 at 2:00pm CT (3:00pm ET)".
- {time}: the time only, like "2:00pm CT".
- {call_link}: the Google Meet link. It can be missing; the system then uses a no-link variant, so write that too: say the link is on their calendar invite.
- {booking_url}: Lorenzo's booking page, for moving the call.
- {slots}: no-show messages only. Two open times, rendered like "Tue 12:05pm or Thu 4:30pm CT".

## Voice (hard rules; Lorenzo has rejected drafts for breaking these)
- The bar test: write it exactly as Lorenzo would text it. Short, plain, casual, human. Contractions are fine.
- Emails: 20 to 70 words, 2 to 4 short paragraphs, "Hey {first_name}," on its own line, "Lorenzo" on its own line at the end. Subjects: short, lower-key, no clickbait.
- Texts: one or two sentences, under 160 characters where possible (320 hard cap), start "Hey {first_name}, Lorenzo here." or similar so an unsaved number is not mistaken for spam.
- No em dashes, no en dashes, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no bullet points.
- Never mention AI, automation, reminders being scheduled, or "this is an automated message".
- Times always with am/pm and CT (the placeholders do this).
- No guessing about their business, no invented facts, no invented client names.
- No fake pressure ("last chance", countdowns). No guilt ("you missed", "I waited"). Things come up.
- Do not repeat another message's opener. Vary wording across the set.
