# Upwork touch walkthrough (started 2026-09-23)

Lorenzo walks through the follow-up touches one at a time, the way section 1
did the emails. Samples are written by hand on a made-up client (no model
spend, no real client data). No prompt or code changes until every touch is
signed off; then all the edits below go in at once, through review.

## The made-up client

- Job post: residential HVAC company, 4 techs, GoHighLevel. Leads from the
  website form and Google LSA. The office manager calls back "when she gets a
  chance", and "a lot of estimates just sit". Wants an instant text on form
  fill, follow-up on unaccepted estimates, and a review request after a job
  closes in Housecall Pro.
- Room: Dana (client) watched the Loom and asked whether it works with
  Housecall Pro or they would have to switch. Lorenzo said it works with
  Housecall Pro and explained how. Dana went quiet.

## Sample drafts shown

- T1 (kicker cut): "Hey Dana, one idea on the estimates that just sit: split
  them by type. Repair quotes get one short text the next morning, replacement
  quotes get a slower follow-up that resends the estimate and asks if they have
  questions. Are most of the ones that sit replacement quotes, or a mix?"
- T2: "Hey Dana, since you asked about Housecall Pro, the part of the Loom
  worth another look is near the end. That's where a job gets closed in
  Housecall Pro and the review request goes out on its own. Here's the link
  again: (Loom link)". The first version ended "Nobody in the office has to
  remember to ask." Lorenzo called that a Claudeism: a line that restates the
  point that was just made.
- T3 v1 (rejected, repeats the proposal's case study): "Hey Dana, your
  estimates that just sit reminded me of a commercial roofing contractor I
  built a routing and follow-up system for. They run $750M+ in annual bid
  volume through it, and every estimate request gets tracked and followed up
  on."
- T3 v2: "Hey Dana, the estimates that just sit remind me of a contractor I
  worked with, very similar setup to yours. I built their follow-up so every
  estimate request got tracked and followed up on."
- T4: "Hey Dana, one thing to watch with the review request after a job closes
  in Housecall Pro: callbacks and warranty visits close as jobs too, so an
  unhappy customer could get asked for a review. I'd only send it on paid jobs
  and skip anything marked as a callback."
- T5: "Hey Dana, quick one on timing: would you want the instant text for new
  form leads live first, or all three pieces at once?"
- T6 (milestone version): "Hey Dana, if it's easier to start small, I could do
  just the instant text on new form leads as a first milestone. The estimate
  follow-up and review requests could come after. Want me to scope it that
  way?"
- T7: "Hey Dana, I'll close this out on my end. If you went another way on
  the Housecall Pro setup, no worries at all. If the estimates are still
  sitting later on, message me here and I'll pick it back up."
- Check-in (Dana had said to come back after the summer rush): "Hey Dana, you
  said to come back once the summer rush was over. One thing I'd add from
  before: the review requests could also go out to the jobs you closed this
  summer, not just new ones. Is the timing better now?"
