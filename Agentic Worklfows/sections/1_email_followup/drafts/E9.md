# E9: A light weekly touch that gives one new, small reason to talk (a true observation about their niche and our offer), then two slots
Timing: One week after E8 (the first of the weekly touches after the "couple of weeks" check-in)

## Version A (closest to Lorenzo's own wording)
Hey {first_name},

Quick thought for you. An account that cancelled or never signed already knows your name, so it's usually an easier conversation than a brand-new lead.

Happy to show you how that would work on your end. How's {slots}? Or grab any time here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version B
Hey {first_name},

An account that cancelled or never signed already knows your name, so it's usually an easier conversation than a brand-new lead.

That's what I'd walk you through on the call. Could you do {slots}? Or pick whatever suits you here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Version C
Hey {first_name},

Here's one thing I'd walk you through on a call. An account that cancelled or never signed already knows your name, so it's usually an easier conversation than a brand-new lead.

I'm free {slots}. Or grab any time here: {booking_url}

Lorenzo

## Pick
B because the new reason is the first thing they see in the preview, with no warm-up line in front of it,
and it's the shortest of the three with no "slots got taken" or "haven't heard back" beat, so it reads like a text from a peer.

## Per-offer lines (only if this email needs them)
Cleaning, reactivation: An account that cancelled or never signed already knows your name, so it's usually an easier conversation than a brand-new lead.
Bookkeeping, reactivation: Quarterly estimates, year-end and tax season each give you a natural reason to check back in with former clients and old inquiries.
Recruiting, reactivation: Clients who hired through you before, or almost did, already know how you work, so they're an easier conversation than any cold account.
Cleaning, outbound (trigger): A company moving into new space has to sort out cleaning around move-in, which is the easiest time to be the one they hear from.
Bookkeeping, outbound (trigger): A company posting a part-time bookkeeper job has already said out loud it needs help with its books, which is the right moment to show up.
Recruiting, outbound: An open specialty role on a job board is a hiring manager saying in public they need someone, which is the right moment to reach them.

## Notes for Lorenzo (optional, max 3 short lines)
The observation sentence swaps per campaign. All three versions show the Cleaning, reactivation line, and each line fits all three versions as written.
Every line is true of any business in that niche. None of them claims anything about the lead or quotes a number.
I left out {company} because the brief gives it no fallback if it's blank. "On your end" and "on the call" do the same job.
