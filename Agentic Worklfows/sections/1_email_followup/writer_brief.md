# Shared brief: the "no reply yet" follow-up sequence (Lorenzo, 2026-09-23)

You are one of several writers. Each writer drafts ONE email of this sequence. A final editor ties them together. Write for Lorenzo Villarreal, founder of AdveroSolutions.

## The situation every email is written for
1. The lead got a cold email (from Instantly) and replied that they are interested.
2. Lorenzo replied to them himself, by hand, and offered two specific call times.
3. The lead has NOT replied since and has NOT booked.
4. From here an automated sequence follows up, in the same email thread (each email is a reply in that thread, so the subject is "Re: <their original subject>"; you don't write subjects).
5. Goal of every email: get a 15-minute call on the calendar. One ask per email.

## The sequence (Lorenzo's own plan; follow it)
Timing is business days after Lorenzo's own reply (Day 0).
- E1, Day 1: "Hey {first_name}, it looks like those two slots got grabbed up. Here are an additional two. Let me know if either of these works better for you instead."
- E2, Day 2: quick research on their company, pertinent to our offer: "Hey {first_name}, I think this call will be really valuable for you in particular due to X, Y and Z." It must be truly applicable to their situation. Two new slots.
- E3, Day 3: "Hey, looks like those slots got grabbed up again. I really want to get this call on the books. Do these two times work?" Two new slots.
- E4, Day 4: proof. Frame: there is a lot of room for growth; "I've done this across numerous industries" and list real results (see Proof). Two new slots.
- E5, Day 5: "Hey, once again, haven't heard back from you. The slots keep getting filled. It is a busy time of year. Wanted to shoot you two more slots and see if either of these work for you."
- E6, Day 6: EITHER a priority check ("Want to make sure this is still a priority for you") OR value ("I put together X for you that I was really excited to show you on the call"). Two new slots.
- E7, Day 7: the last one of the daily week. Lorenzo left this open ("some sort of campaign from that, you get the point").
- Then the cadence slows down.
- E8, two weeks after E7: "Hey {first_name}, been a couple of weeks. Was hoping now was a good time to get a call on the books. Here are two slots. Let me know if right now is a bad time, or if this isn't a priority."
- E9 and E10, weekly after E8: "a couple more times", then the lead moves to a 90-day nurture campaign (not part of this job).

## Voice (hard rules; Lorenzo has rejected drafts for breaking these)
- The bar test: write it exactly as Lorenzo would say it out loud or text it. Short, plain, casual, human. Contractions are fine.
- 25 to 80 words per email (E4 proof may go to about 100). Mobile-first: 2 to 4 short paragraphs.
- Open with "Hey {first_name}," on its own line. Sign off "Lorenzo" on its own line. No title, no P.S. unless it is the proof list.
- No em dashes, no en dashes, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no bullet points except a proof list in E4.
