# Openers: recruiting agencies (12 human variants per segment)

The opener is the first sentence after `{{personalization}}`. Written by hand first (blueprint M3.2), then 3 of each set are spun into `{{RANDOM | ... }}` in `build_sequences.py`. Any of these can replace a spin option; keep the proof line and the guarantee untouched. No em dashes, no exclamation marks, peer voice. Recruiting is on a 1,000-send verdict (blueprint M4): if positives stay at zero, retire both campaigns rather than rewrite them.

## Segment: recruiting_reactivation (percentage of placement fees on accounts brought back)

1. I build client reactivation systems for recruiting agencies.
2. Quick one: the hiring managers who used you once and went quiet still have open reqs, they just stopped hearing from you.
3. Straight to it: I bring back dormant client accounts for agencies like yours, and I only get paid on placements that come from it.
4. Here is the short version: sequences to every client that placed once or asked for a search and never came back.
5. Odd question, but how many of your past client accounts did anyone email in the last year?
6. Wondering if you would take a percentage-of-fee deal on accounts you have already written off.
7. Not sure if this fits, but if you have a list of clients who went quiet after one placement, that list is where I work.
8. Quick thought: a client who used you once already trusts you, which is the expensive part of every new account.
9. Small idea with no cost attached: let me work the accounts that went quiet and only pay me on the placements that follow.
10. Most agencies have 30 to 100 client accounts that placed once and vanished, and almost nobody follows up with them twice.
11. Every agency has near-miss clients, the ones who asked for a search and went with someone else, and those are the easiest second chances.
12. Fair warning, this is a cold note, but it is about clients you already had and lost.

## Segment: recruiting_outbound (guaranteed $40,000 in placement fees in 180 days)

1. I build outbound systems for recruiting agencies.
2. Quick one: every {{specialty}} role that has sat open on a job board for three weeks is a hiring manager who is stuck.
3. Straight to it: I find the stuck reqs in your specialty, reach the hiring manager under your name, and put the intro call on your calendar.
4. Here is the short version: open reqs in your market, contacted under your name, intro calls booked for you.
5. Odd question, but who tells you when a {{specialty}} role in your market passes the thirty-day mark unfilled?
6. Every week there are {{specialty}} roles in your market that have been open for a month, and nobody is calling those managers.
7. I run outreach for agencies like yours: stuck req, hiring manager, intro call on your calendar.
8. Wondering if you would want the intro calls booked for you instead of cold calling for them.
9. Quick thought: a req that has sat open for six weeks is a manager who would take a recruiter's call today.
10. Not sure if this fits, but I fill recruiter calendars with hiring managers who already have a req and a budget.
11. Small idea: I pull the reqs in your specialty that just crossed thirty days and put you in front of the manager the same week.
12. Fair warning, this is a cold note, but it comes with a written guarantee on placement fees.
