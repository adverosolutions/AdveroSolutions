# Openers: bookkeeping and tax (12 human variants per segment)

The opener is the first sentence after `{{personalization}}`. Written by hand first (blueprint M3.2), then 3 of each set are spun into `{{RANDOM | ... }}` in `build_sequences.py`. Any of these can replace a spin option; keep the proof line and the guarantee untouched. No em dashes, no exclamation marks, peer voice.

## Segment: bookkeeping_reactivation (percentage of first-year fees on clients brought back)

1. I build client reactivation systems for bookkeeping and tax firms.
2. Quick one: the people who asked you for a quote last tax season and never signed are the cheapest clients you will ever win.
3. Straight to it: I bring back former clients and lost inquiries for firms like yours, and I only get paid on the ones who return.
4. Here is the short version: sequences to every lost inquiry and former client, timed to tax season, quarterly estimates, and year-end cleanup.
5. Odd question, but how many former clients did anyone at your firm email in the last twelve months?
6. Between now and year-end cleanup is the best window all year to bring back the clients who drifted.
7. Wondering if you would take a percentage-of-fees deal on clients you have already written off.
8. Not sure if this fits, but if you have a list of people who filed once and disappeared, that list is where I work.
9. Quick thought: a client who left after one season usually left over a small thing, and a well-timed note brings them back.
10. Small idea with no cost attached: let me work your lapsed clients and only pay me on first-year fees from whoever returns.
11. Every firm has a stack of one-time filers and unanswered quotes, and almost nobody follows up with them twice.
12. Fair warning, this is a cold note, but it is about clients you already had and lost.

## Segment: bookkeeping_trigger (guaranteed $15,000 in new annual fees in 90 days)

1. I build outbound acquisition systems for bookkeeping and tax firms.
2. Quick one: new LLCs in your area pick a bookkeeper in their first 90 days, usually whoever reaches them first.
3. Straight to it: I watch new business filings and second-location openings, then reach the owner under your name.
4. Here is the short version: trigger-based outreach to new businesses and companies hiring part-time bookkeepers, with the discovery calls landing on your calendar.
5. Odd question, but who tells you when a company near you posts a part-time bookkeeper job?
6. Every week new businesses get filed in your county and most of them still do their own books for the first year.
7. I run outreach for firms like yours: new filing, new location, or an admin job post, then a discovery call on your calendar.
8. Wondering if you would want the discovery calls booked for you instead of waiting on referrals.
9. Quick thought: a company posting a part-time bookkeeper role is telling you it needs a firm, not a hire.
10. Not sure if this fits, but I fill bookkeeping calendars with owners who just opened their doors.
11. Small idea: I find the businesses that formed this month and put you in front of them before their first quarterly.
12. Fair warning, this is a cold note, but it comes with a written guarantee on new annual fees.
