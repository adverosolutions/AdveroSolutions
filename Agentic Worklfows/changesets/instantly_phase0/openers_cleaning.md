# Openers: cleaning (12 human variants per segment)

The opener is the first sentence after `{{personalization}}`. Written by hand first (blueprint M3.2), then 3 of each set are spun into `{{RANDOM | ... }}` in `build_sequences.py`. Any of these can replace a spin option; keep the proof line and the guarantee untouched. No em dashes, no exclamation marks, peer voice.

## Segment: cleaning_reactivation (commission on accounts brought back)

1. Know this is out of left field, but if I could recover the money from accounts that cancelled or stopped renewing, in exchange for a commission on the ones I bring back, would you be open to it?
2. Out of left field, I know, but what if the accounts that stopped renewing last year came back without anyone on your team chasing them?
3. Odd question, but how many of the contracts you lost in the last two years did anyone follow up with more than once?
4. Quick one: I get paid to bring back cleaning accounts that cancelled, and only when they actually come back.
5. Straight to it: most cleaning companies have a drawer of proposals nobody ever said no to, and that drawer is where I work.
6. Here is the short version: I chase your former accounts and lost bids on commission, and the ones that are ready book a walkthrough on your calendar.
7. This is a strange one, but the cheapest new contract you will sign this year is one you already had and lost.
8. Not sure if this fits, but if you have accounts that walked after a price bump or a bad month, I bring those back on a percentage.
9. Quick thought: the buildings that cancelled on you still need cleaning, they just stopped hearing from you.
10. Wondering if you would take a commission-only deal on accounts you have already written off.
11. Small idea with no cost attached: let me work the accounts that went quiet and only pay me on what returns.
12. Fair warning, this is a cold note, but it is about money you already earned once and lost.

## Segment: cleaning_trigger (guaranteed $24,000/yr in 90 days)

1. I build outbound acquisition systems for commercial cleaning companies.
2. Quick one: I find the buildings that just signed a lease or switched property managers and get you the walkthrough before anyone else bids.
3. Straight to it: new leases in your area get a cleaning contract within a few weeks, and I make sure you are the first bid in the door.
4. Here is the short version: I watch for expansions, new construction, and management changes, then reach the decision maker under your name.
5. Odd question, but who tells you when a 40,000 square foot office in your area changes hands?
6. Every month a handful of companies near you sign a new lease, and most of them pick a cleaner in the first thirty days.
7. I run trigger-based outreach for cleaning companies: new lease, new floor, new property manager, then a walkthrough on your calendar.
8. Wondering if you would want the walkthroughs booked for you instead of chasing them.
9. Quick thought: the contracts you lose to competitors were usually decided before you knew the building existed.
10. Not sure if this fits, but I fill cleaning calendars with walkthroughs from buildings that just changed hands.
11. Small idea: I find the property managers who just took over a building and put you in front of them the same week.
12. Fair warning, this is a cold note, but it comes with a written guarantee on new contracts.
