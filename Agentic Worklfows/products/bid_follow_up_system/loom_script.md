# Loom script: the bid follow-up system (3 minutes)

Client-facing. No price is spoken. Screen: the tracker sheet, then the workflow canvas, then the sheet again.

---

**0:00 to 0:25. Their own number, said back to them.**

Hi, this is Lorenzo. You send out quotes every week. Say the number out loud, how many are sitting there right now with no answer.

That is not a closing problem. Most of those people meant to reply and then a week went by.

**0:25 to 0:55. What happens today.**

Today, chasing depends on somebody remembering. It happens on a slow Tuesday and it does not happen in a busy week, which is exactly the week you sent the most quotes. So the good weeks leak the most.

The other thing that happens is the chase is awkward, because after ten days it feels like begging. So it does not get sent at all.

**0:55 to 1:45. What this does. [screen: the sheet]**

Here is the tracker. Every quote you send gets a row. The moment it lands, an eight-touch sequence starts behind it, spread over two weeks. Some of them are emails, some are texts, all of them come from you, in your words.

Day one is the quote landing. Day three asks whether anything needs adjusting. Day seven acknowledges they are probably comparing. Day fourteen closes the file and says the price holds for thirty days. That last one gets more replies than anything else in the sequence, because it lets them off the hook.

**1:45 to 2:20. The part that matters. [screen: a replied row]**

The moment they reply, by text or by email, the whole thing stops. You do not chase someone who already answered. That one rule is the difference between this being useful and it being the reason a customer stops picking up.

And you get told. A text and an email, straight away, with the quote they are replying about.

**2:20 to 2:45. Who owns it. [screen: the Templates tab]**

The words live in this sheet, not buried somewhere you cannot reach. You want to change day seven, you change it here and the next quote uses the new version. You want to stop chasing someone, you change their status and it stops. It is yours.

**2:45 to 3:00. The ask.**

If you send me three of the quotes you have sent in the last month, and how you would normally chase them, I will show you what the first two weeks would have looked like. Takes me a day.

Thanks for watching. Lorenzo.

## Recording notes
