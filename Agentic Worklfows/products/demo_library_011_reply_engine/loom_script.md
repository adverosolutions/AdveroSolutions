# Loom script: the reply and booking engine, Demo Library 011 (3 minutes)

Used in Upwork proposals where the job is reply handling or speed to lead. No price is spoken. Screen: the canvas, then a draft on a phone, then a calendar.

---

**0:00 to 0:25. The number that decides everything.**

Hi, this is Lorenzo. You already know the number: answer inside five minutes and you are talking to them, answer in an hour and you are one of four people they are ignoring.

The problem is that replies do not arrive when you are free. They arrive on site, in a meeting, or at 9pm.

**0:25 to 1:00. What this watches for. [screen: the canvas]**

This is the engine. A reply comes in here, and the first thing it does is sort it. Interested, a question, not now, wrong person, out of office, or somebody telling you to get lost.

Those last two matter as much as the first. Out of office parks them until they are back rather than burning a touch. Somebody telling you to get lost never hears from you again, same day, no reply, off the list. That is the one everybody builds last and it is the one that keeps you out of trouble.

**1:00 to 1:45. The answer. [screen: the draft]**

For an interested reply it writes the answer. It answers their actual question first, in about two sentences, then offers two real openings from your calendar.

Real openings, with rules on them. Afternoons only. At least four hours out, so you never get an appointment you cannot make. Inside the next week. And never a slot it already offered to somebody else that morning, which is the mistake that costs you a customer on your first double booking.

Anything it cannot answer from your own approved sheet, it will not answer. It will not invent a price.

**1:45 to 2:20. The approval. [screen: the phone]**

Here is the part I would argue for. The first answer to an interested person does not send by itself. It arrives on your phone, written, with approve or change on it. Two taps.

After the first one, the follow-ups run on their own for a week. A touch a day, then it stops.

Two taps is not a limitation, it is what makes this safe to turn on at all. When you trust it, the approval comes off.

**2:20 to 2:45. What you actually see. [screen: the calendar]**

The answer goes back in the same thread, from the same address, inside working hours. The pipeline row moves. If they took a time, it is on your calendar with the reminders already running.

**2:45 to 3:00. The ask.**

Send me the last ten replies you got and how you answered them. I will show you which ones this would have handled on its own, which ones it would have asked you about, and which ones it would have left alone.

Thanks for watching. Lorenzo.

## Recording notes

- The hostile and out-of-office branches are what separates this from every other demo. Thirty seconds on them.
- The approval step is a feature. Say so plainly, do not apologise for it.
- Show a real double-booking rule. Prospects who have been burned lean in there.
- No price on the video.
