A Content Marketing Philosophy Routed In Pragmatism In the mid-1990s, I was skeptical of experts who said the internet was going to transform everything. At the time, the only thing it seemed to change was the way stuff was sold. Remember Pets.com? Well, I was wrong. A revolution is taking place that's as powerful as the industrial revolution in the late 1800s. The web and mobile technologies have given consumers the power to know more about a service or company than at any other time in our history. Google's research indicates that buyers are using online tools as part of their decision-making process on every single step towards a transaction. Often, prospects interact with a company more than thirty times before a sale. Data-driven marketing and advertising are become a highly personalized experience across every connected device, from a laptop to an iPhone to Alexa. Ignoring this shift means a firm's identity (and earnings) will mirror brands like JC Penney, AOL, and BlackBerry. Now is the time to structure a marketing plan that cuts through the sludge of typical advertising and attracts new clients! Content Marketing and the Grateful Dead's Influence The best way to attract people online is through free education, creative content, and making a personal connection. It's called content marketing, inbound marketing, and as Seth Godin has coined it, permission-based marketing. I learned this practice firsthand, way before Esther Dyson discussed the idea in a seminal 1994 Wired article that spawned a million tech start-ups. Hubspot may be the most famous company to co-opt (and corrupt) the idea. In 1994 I was in my 7th year working as a freelancer for Grateful Dead Merchandising with a vast bootleg tape collection and 120+ shows seen in venues as distant as the Stockholm Globe Arena Greek Theater in Berkeley, CA. The Dead's marketing concept was simple, give fans access to their music for free by allowing them to tape shows and share those tapes with other fans. The result of disseminating all of this free content/music was the development of a passionate group of fans who purchased everything the Grateful Dead chose to make available for sale.
