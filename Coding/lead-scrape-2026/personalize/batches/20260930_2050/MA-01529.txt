ABOUT US - Incredible Towns Skip to content Welcome to Incredible Towns. Here's a little bit about us. Choya and Laura Harden - Co-Owners - Incredible Towns - Incredible Business Networking - Incredible Business Speed Networking - Incredible Visibility Where we came from, and where we are going, this is our why. Sometimes you need to read the writing on the wall and be willing to make a change. We have done this multiple times in our working life, and it has helped build our professional pride and joy, Incredible Towns, and Incredible Business Networking (IBN). We hope you enjoy reading our story, and take from it the importance of having a willingness to adapt. From rural Wisconsin to Incredible Towns … Our first moment of change came during the Great Recession of 2007. My wife and I owned a farm-to-table restaurant in rural southwest Wisconsin. I had been in restaurants for a while before that and worked about every job you could have, from dishwasher on up. We were open seven days a week for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, and made many of our dishes from scratch. There were many times we would be at the restaurant from 4 a.m. until midnight, come home, take a nap, and go right back. When you own a restaurant, you're always there. Unfortunately the recession in 2007 hit us hard and we had to close. My wife, Laura, and I decided to relocate. When evaluating our options, we had several criteria on our checklist: a good climate, good food, access to the outdoors, and professional opportunities. After doing some research online, we decided on Asheville, NC and we moved there on a prayer. I worked at a couple of different jobs and then took a position selling advertising with the Yellow Pages. One day, the Vice President of the entire company flew into North Carolina and assembled everyone who worked for Yellow Pages in the state for a meeting in Hickory. I'll never forget this meeting. His big announcement was that the Yellow Pages, which was a print company, was going to start selling Internet ads and then give away ad space in the print version of the Yellow Pages for free, according to how much online advertising that client bought. I turned in my notice that afternoon and told my manager that they would be out of business within the year. Six months later, that company went bankrupt!
