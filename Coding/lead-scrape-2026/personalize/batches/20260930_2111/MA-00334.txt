About | Kimber Oak Skip to content Our Story A better way to approach business gifting was hiding in plain sight. How It Started From one Christmas ornament to a different kind of gifting company. Before Kimber Oak worked with leading brands, it began with a handmade ornament and the belief that business gifts could be worth keeping, talking about, and remembering. Before Kimber Oak, our founder, Kade Kimber, spent years in corporate marketing sending gifts on behalf of the companies he worked for. Branded merchandise. Generic gift cards. The occasional fruit basket. He watched companies spend real money on gifts that were quickly forgotten, rarely talked about, and more obligatory than memorable. Companies were spending, but the gifts just weren t doing enough once they reached the recipient. Kade knew there had to be a better way to approach business gifting. He just never expected the answer to begin with a Christmas ornament. In 2013, Kade was working on a personal Christmas project: a custom ornament he had imagined but couldn’t find anywhere. So he decided to make it himself, learning glass etching and sandblasting along the way. Not long after, during a casual conversation while shopping locally, Kade shared a photo of the ornament with a boutique owner. She asked if he would consider selling them in her store. He walked in to make a purchase and walked out with a lease for retail space inside the boutique. From there, he started applying the same sandblasting techniques to custom drinkware. Around that time, a chance conversation with a top-performing realtor helped everything click. The better version of corporate gifting he had been looking for was already in his hands. What began as etched glassware quickly grew into a local gifting business. A magazine editor spotted the pieces in the boutique and featured them in a local publication, which caught the attention of a buyer for the U.S. Women s Open. Just three months after opening, Kade was creating gifts for players at that year s tournament. For the first time in his career, he saw gifting do what companies always hoped it would do. These were not throwaway gifts. They were gifts people kept, displayed, and talked about. In the early days, glassware orders were sandblasted on his back patio, no matter the weather.
