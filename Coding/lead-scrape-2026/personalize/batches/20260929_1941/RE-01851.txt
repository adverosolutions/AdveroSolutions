A new sales rep’s first ninety days decide whether the hire worked. Most of that window goes to things that are not selling. Building a prospect list. Figuring out what a cold email should say, then rewriting it four times. Working out a pitch by trial and error on live prospects. Learning what to do after somebody says maybe. By the time all of that gets sorted out, a quarter is gone, and the rep is only then starting on the job you hired them to do. Some of them get there. Some of them run out of runway first. That stretch is the real cost of a sales hire, and nobody sells you a solution to it. It is the part I do. What We Do We Recruit the Rep. I Build the System They Work From. Two things, and they come together as one program. First, we recruit you a sales or business development representative. Then I build the outbound system that rep works from — the list, the copy, the pitch, the call process — and I train them on it personally, until they can run the whole thing without me. That is why your rep spends their first ninety days in front of buyers instead of in a spreadsheet. The rep. We recruit local, remote, and international candidates — wherever the right person for the job happens to be. Compensation runs a wide range. Remote reps start around $15,000 in Latin America and around $50,000 in the United States. A local outside sales hire in most industries runs well into six figures . Tell us the role and what you want to spend, and we will show you people who fit it. We screen every candidate before you ever see them. And I have hired and trained these reps inside my own company, not just for clients, so we know what a good one looks like at day ninety and not just in an interview.
