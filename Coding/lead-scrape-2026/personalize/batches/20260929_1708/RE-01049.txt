How it started Our Story How a painful encounter with a sea urchin led to a realization that outsourcing is broken. In 2022, I stepped on a sea urchin while visiting my team in the Philippines. Sitting in the ER (and laughing at my $3.41 medical bill) something clicked for me: growing a business is kinda like stepping on a sea urchin—it s honestly super painful. Startups, agencies and mid-sized enterprises comprise 99% of U.S. companies. But, it’s never been harder to grow and compete in the global economy. During my time at Goldman Sachs, I realized multinational corporations have an unfair competitive advantage—access to top skilled talent all over the world. After talking to hundreds of founders, I see the same pain-points every time: constant fire-drills, long to-do lists, and massive bottlenecks. Execution can t keep up, cash is a constraint, and building a full U.S. team is expensive and takes years to fully execute. It s like stepping on a sea urchin every single day. After drowning in tasks, I built and managed a team of incredible overseas operators. These in-house teammates owned customer service, product operations, and sales enablement—and the results were fantastic. Clients had no idea these teammates weren t physically working in our U.S. office. Our startup executed better, supported clients 24/7, and U.S. teammates were finally able to breathe and focus on the real strategic initiatives that moved the business forward. And, we never lost a single teammate—100% retention. That experience changed my view on how we do outsourcing. But outsourcing can be awful, and it s hard to know who to trust. The problem isn t offshore, it s the approach of only treating offshore hiring like a call center. When businesses treat offshoring like a call center, you get the same outcomes every time: low retention, low ownership, and frustrated customers.
