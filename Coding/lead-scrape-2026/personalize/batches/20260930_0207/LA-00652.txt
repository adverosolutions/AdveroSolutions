Support our cause About Re:wild Your Campus Re:wild Your Campus is a national nonprofit movement transforming college and university grounds into vibrant, healthy places where people, pollinators, and ecosystems thrive. We’re driven by a bold vision: eliminate synthetic pesticides from all U.S. campuses by 2030 and replace them with regenerative, organic land-care practices that protect human health, restore biodiversity, and build climate resilience. We are so excited to announce the premiere of Grassroots: The Re:wild Your Campus Story, a short film by Palmer Morse and Joshua Izenberg. It follows the movement across Grinnell College, Drexel University, and UCLA, and traces it all the way back to where it began: UC Berkeley, where co-founder Mackenzie Feldman launched Herbicide Free Cal as a student athlete in 2017. This film carries years of work, relationship building, memories, challenges, and a belief in doing things differently. It’s a reflection of the many students, groundskeepers, and communities who made this movement possible. Thank you to our executive producers Joel David Moore and Mark Bittman. We have a singular and powerful focus: the wild as the most effective solution to the interconnected climate, biodiversity and human health crises. Founded by a group of renowned conservation scientists together with Leonardo DiCaprio, Re:wild is a force multiplier that brings together Indigenous peoples, local communities, influential leaders, nongovernmental organizations, governments, companies and the public to protect and rewild at the scale and speed we need. Re:wild launched in 2021 combining more than three decades of conservation impact by Leonardo DiCaprio and Global Wildlife Conservation, leveraging expertise, partnerships and platforms to bring new attention, energy and voices together. Our vital work has protected and conserved over 12 million acres benefitting more than 16,000 species in the world’s most irreplaceable places for biodiversity. We don’t need to reinvent the planet. We just need to rewild it—for all wildkind.
