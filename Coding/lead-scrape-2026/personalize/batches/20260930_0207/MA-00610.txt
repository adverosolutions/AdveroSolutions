Your Buyers Are Asking Questions About Your Business. Where Are the Answers Coming From? Hiring an outside marketing guide...is well worth it. Tom Repp Sep 28 2026 B2B content marketing, digital marketing for B2B industrial, LLM and B2B industrial marketing Your buyers have questions. Which material is best for this application? What causes this component to fail? What should I look for in a supplier? Who really knows this niche? For decades, buyers took those questions to salespeople, distributors, trade publications, Google and trusted colleagues. Now they are also asking AI. According to the 2026 State of Marketing to Engineers report, 69% of technical buyers use generative AI at least occasionally to help evaluate vendors or research products and services. At the same time, 62% of the technical buying process happens online before buyers engage with sales. That creates an important question for industrial companies: When AI answers questions about your industry ... where are those answers coming from? And is your company's expertise part of the conversation? How Do LLMs Work? The Simple Version Large language models, or LLMs, are AI systems trained on enormous amounts of text. During training, they learn patterns and relationships in language. Given a prompt, the model generates a response based on those learned patterns. That's the ridiculously simplified explanation. There's another wrinkle that matters to marketers. Some AI systems can also retrieve current information from the web or other connected sources when answering questions. In those situations, the system isn't relying solely on what it learned during training. It may find current information, synthesize it and sometimes provide citations. Maybe from your B2B? So, there are two concepts worth separating. What an LLM learned during training is one thing. What an AI-powered answer engine can find today is another. The technology is complicated. The business implication isn't. AI needs information to work with. Your brand's information. AI Can't Read Your Engineer's Mind Industrial companies have an interesting problem. Your engineer may have spent 25 years solving a specific manufacturing problem. Your sales manager may know exactly why one application succeeds and another fails. That expertise has tremendous value. But if it lives inside people's heads, email chains and sales conversations, it is largely invisible to the outside world. Your company knows it.
