About — A Boutique Advisory for IEEPA Recovery · Global Profit Advisors Skip to content About the Firm Built for a finite regulatory moment. Global Profit Advisors is a boutique advisory firm organized specifically to handle IEEPA duty recovery under the CAPE Declaration framework. We are not a customs brokerage, a law firm, or a tax practice. We are an operational partner to both — with one engagement and one outcome in mind. Independent · Contingency-aligned · CBP-process literate What We Are, and Are Not Narrow by design. Refund recovery at scale is a specific discipline. It sits between the legal analysis customs and trade attorneys perform and the day-to-day entry filing customs brokers perform — and it does not look like either. We built the firm to live in that specific place. We are An operational partner for refund recovery Specialists in CBP post-entry declaration work Contingency-aligned with our clients and broker partners Direct, candid, and document-driven in everything we ship We are not A customs brokerage — your broker remains the filer A law firm — we retain counsel where the work requires it A lead-gen shop — every engagement is actively run, not passed through A promotional operation — we decline cases we cannot meaningfully help with Principles How we run the practice. 01 Document everything. Every assertion in every filing is backed by primary-source documentation. If a number is in the declaration, it is traceable to a 7501, an invoice, or a payment record. There is no other way to do this work. 02 Align incentives. We only benefit when our clients benefit. The engagement is structured so that our outcome is tied to the outcome on the filing — not the volume of filings, not the hours billed, not the deck pages produced. 03 Respect the license. Your customs broker s license is the gating piece of this process. We never operate in a way that puts it at risk, and we never position ourselves as a substitute for the filer of record. 04 Say the quiet part. If a case is not recoverable, we say so. If a timeline is unrealistic, we say so. If a number is not defensible, we say so. The alternative is to over-promise early and under-deliver later — a model other firms can run if they want to.
