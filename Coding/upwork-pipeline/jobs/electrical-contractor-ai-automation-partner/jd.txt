We want one agency to plan, build, and support AI and automation across our company. This document describes the work, how we will start, our requirements, and what to include in your proposal. Please send proposals and questions through Upwork.
1. About us
We are a commercial electrical contractor with about 130 employees and five locations in Maryland and Virginia. Our work covers lighting service and maintenance, construction, roadway and highway lighting, traffic signals, and sports lighting. Our customers include commercial property owners and managers, shopping centers, and public agencies.
2. Systems in use
- Microsoft 365: Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, and OneDrive
- CompanyCam for job photos
- Company website, Facebook, LinkedIn, and Google Business Profiles
- AI phone answering platform we are finalizing for our main number and five local numbers
- Accounting, payroll, fleet, telematics, and job-management systems are out of scope for Phase 1. Build Phase 1 work inside Microsoft 365 so it can connect to our job-management system later.
3. Scope: eight areas
3.1 Lead and request intake
Goal: Every lead and service request lands in one place, reaches the right person within minutes, and never goes unanswered.
- One shared mailbox receives emails, website forms, and phone-call summaries.
- AI sorts each message (new lead, service request, existing job, vendor or sales pitch, junk) and pulls out the name, company, site address, location or division, and urgency.
- The right person or division gets a Teams alert, and every item is logged in a tracker with an owner and a status.
- An automatic reply confirms receipt and says who is handling the request.
- Anything unanswered past a set time is escalated, and a weekly summary shows volume and response times.
Success looks like: time to first response, share of messages routed correctly, and no lost leads.
3.2 AI phone system
Goal: Implement the platform we select across our main number and five local numbers.
- Help configure routing by location and time of day, English and Spanish intake, and emergency escalation that requires an on-call person to confirm. A ring or a voicemail does not count as confirmation.
- Send call summaries and transcripts into the intake process in 3.1.
Success looks like: every call answered, logged, and routed, and every emergency confirmed by a person.
3.3 Website
Goal: The website answers common questions and captures requests at any hour.
- An AI assistant limited to approved content: our services, service area, and how to request work.
- Request forms that feed the intake process in 3.1.
- Written recommendations for the site itself: mobile experience, speed, location pages, and calls to action.
Success looks like: more requests captured through the website and fewer basic questions reaching staff.
3.4 Email
Goal: Less junk, and important messages stand out.
- Tune Microsoft 365 spam and bulk-mail filtering, including stricter settings for leadership mailboxes.
- Set up inbox triage for leadership: rules, views, and AI prioritization where it pays for itself.
Success looks like: a measurable drop in junk reaching leadership, with no legitimate mail lost.
3.5 Microsoft 365 Copilot
Goal: Decide with data who should have Copilot.
- Run a 60-day pilot with 5 to 10 heavy email and document users, with training and before-and-after time estimates.
- Recommend who should get licenses and on which plan.
Success looks like: a clear decision to keep, expand, or drop.
3.6 Marketing automation
Goal: Tools and workflows that our in-house marketing staff will run. You build and train; our staff operate.
- Scheduled Facebook and LinkedIn posting with an approval step.
- A workflow that turns CompanyCam job photos into approved project posts.
- Google Business Profiles kept current for each location.
- Email marketing for customer newsletters and seasonal campaigns, and rotating messages in our email signatures.
Success looks like: consistent posting on a schedule, with approvals, that does not depend on one person remembering.
3.7 Time-savers across departments
Goal: Find and fix the biggest time drains in the company.
- Interview service, construction, estimating, accounting, HR, fleet, and administration.
- Rank the opportunities by hours saved, cost, and risk, then build the top items.
- Use features we already pay for before building anything new (for example, CompanyCam's AI field reports).
Success looks like: hours saved per week, checked 60 days after each rollout.
3.8 Training, documentation, and policy
- Short training for each rollout, recorded where possible.
- Documentation for every automation: what it does, where it lives, how to fix it, and what it costs each month.
- A written AI acceptable-use policy for employees that covers what data may and may not go into AI tools.
- A clean handoff, so we can run and maintain the work without you.
4. How we will start
Phase 1: Discovery and roadmap (fixed price, about 2 to 3 weeks)
- Interviews and workflow mapping, with actual volumes (emails, calls, and leads per week).
- Quick wins completed, such as spam and bulk-mail tuning.
- A written roadmap showing priority, cost, licensing, estimated hours saved, and timeline for each item.
Phase 2: First build (fixed price with milestones)
- Most likely lead and request intake (3.1), unless discovery shows a better first project.
- Build, test, train, and document, followed by 30 days of support.
Phase 3: The rest of the roadmap
- Separate fixed-price projects or a monthly retainer, only if Phases 1 and 2 go well.
5. Requirements
- Microsoft 365 first. If another tool is the better choice, explain why and what it costs per month.
- We own everything. All work is built in our Microsoft 365 tenant and company-owned accounts. Nothing we depend on may live in an agency or personal account.
- Least-privilege access through dedicated accounts with multifactor authentication. We can remove access at any time.
- Customer-facing AI never promises arrival times, prices, authorizations, contract coverage, or completed repairs.
- U.S.-based team members for anything that touches our systems or customer data. NDA required.
- Named team members for each area, and disclosure of any subcontractors.
- Plain-English status updates every week.
- A day or two on-site in Maryland or Virginia during discovery is a plus.
6. What to include in your proposal
- Which of the eight areas your team does in-house, which you would subcontract, and which is your weakest.
- Two or three comparable projects that have run in production for at least six months, with client references available for finalists. Experience with trades, field service, or construction companies is a plus.
- The named people who would do the work and their roles.
- Your fixed price and timeline for Phase 1, and a price range for Phase 2.
- The monthly software and licensing costs we should expect after handoff.
7. How we will choose
We will weigh proof of production work, depth in each area, the approach to security and ownership, clear communication, and price. Finalists will meet with us by video call before we hire.
