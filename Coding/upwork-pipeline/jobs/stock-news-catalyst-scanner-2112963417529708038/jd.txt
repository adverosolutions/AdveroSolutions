Description:
We are looking for an experienced Python developer to build a real-time U.S. stock monitoring and news-analysis system for discretionary day trading.

This is not an automated trading bot. The system will not place trades or determine technical entries.

Its purpose is to identify stocks that are moving because of fresh, meaningful news, analyze the underlying catalyst, and alert the trader quickly. The trader will independently review the chart and wait for their own technical setup before entering a trade.

The system will focus on three types of opportunities:

1. Small-Cap Short Watch
Identify small-cap stocks making significant moves where a fresh catalyst exists, but the substance of the news may be weak, promotional, vague, non-binding, financially insignificant, or disproportionate to the stock's price reaction.
Examples may include partnerships with no disclosed financial value, vague AI announcements, preliminary results, non-binding agreements, or announcements with no clear revenue impact.
The system should flag these as potential short-watch candidates, not automatic short signals.

2. Small-Cap Long Watch
Identify small-cap stocks making significant moves supported by fresh and materially strong news.
Examples may include meaningful contracts, strong clinical/FDA developments, acquisitions, strong earnings/guidance, significant financing/business developments, or other genuinely material catalysts.

3. Large-Cap Long Watch
Identify larger U.S. companies experiencing significant moves based on strong fresh catalysts such as earnings surprises, raised guidance, material contracts, M&A, FDA/regulatory developments, or other major company-specific news.
Large-cap stocks should not be selected simply because they are moving. A meaningful catalyst should be verified.

AI Catalyst Analysis
The AI component should analyze the actual underlying news, press release, or filing whenever possible — not simply classify a headline.
We are considering models/APIs from:
*OpenAI
*Anthropic
*Google

The architecture should preferably be model-independent, allowing us to benchmark or replace the AI provider without rebuilding the entire application.
AI should be used for qualitative catalyst analysis.
Deterministic Python logic should handle numeric calculations, market-data filters, timestamps, security eligibility, and other objective rules whenever possible.
For each catalyst, the system should attempt to determine items such as:
- Publication timestamp and freshness
- Original source
- Catalyst category
- Materiality
- Contract/value disclosed or not
- Potential revenue impact
- Binding vs non-binding agreement
- Clinical/FDA significance where applicable
- Offering/dilution risk
- Recent reverse split or important corporate action
- Promotional/vague wording
- Whether old news is being recycled
- Whether the stock reaction appears disproportionate to the substance of the catalyst

The AI should return a structured classification such as:
STRONG CATALYST
Potential Long Watch
POTENTIAL PUFFY / WEAK CATALYST
Potential Short Watch
MIXED / UNCLEAR
Requires manual review
NO FRESH CATALYST
No current verifiable company-specific catalyst found
Every classification should include the evidence/reasoning used to reach it.

Critical Requirement: No False Catalyst Matching
This is extremely important.
If a stock is moving strongly but the system cannot verify a fresh relevant catalyst, it must explicitly report:
NO FRESH CATALYST
The system must not attach an old, unrelated, or recycled article to today's price movement simply because the ticker appears in the article.
News freshness should be based on reliable timestamps and original-source verification whenever possible.

Alerts
Breaking material news should be analyzed as quickly as practical rather than waiting for a scheduled market scan.
